How it works

Understanding Education Loan EMI

An education loan is disbursed in stages during your course, and repayment (EMI) typically starts only after a moratorium period — the course duration plus a grace period of 6-12 months after completion or landing a job, whichever comes first. Unlike other loans, no EMI is due during this moratorium, but interest usually still accrues.

Formula used: Capitalized Principal = P + (P × annual rate ÷ 100 × moratorium months ÷ 12), where the accrued simple interest during moratorium is added to the principal. EMI is then calculated on this capitalized principal using EMI = P′ × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1) over the repayment tenure.

Worked example: an ₹8,00,000 loan at 10.5% p.a. with a 30-month moratorium accrues about ₹1,75,000 in interest, capitalizing the principal to roughly ₹9,75,000. Repaid over 8 years (96 months), this gives an EMI of approximately ₹15,054.

Why this differs from other EMI calculators: the moratorium period means your effective loan amount at the start of repayment is higher than what was originally disbursed, since unpaid interest compounds into the principal — this calculator accounts for that capitalization before computing your EMI.

This calculator is for educational planning purposes only. Actual EMI may vary depending on your lender's specific moratorium and capitalization policy; confirm the precise figure with your bank or NBFC.

If you (or your family) can afford to pay the interest as it accrues during the moratorium — even in small amounts — the principal stays at the originally disbursed amount instead of growing through capitalization, which meaningfully lowers your EMI once regular repayment begins. Many lenders also offer a 0.5-1% interest rate concession for borrowers who service interest during the moratorium, compounding the savings further.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes When Planning an Education Loan

1. Ignoring interest accrual during the moratorium. Many students assume "no EMI" means "no cost" during the course — but interest typically still accrues and capitalizes, quietly inflating the loan you'll eventually repay.

2. Not exploring the Section 80E tax deduction. Interest paid on an education loan is fully deductible from taxable income for up to 8 years — factor this tax benefit into your real cost-of-borrowing comparison.

3. Borrowing the maximum sanctioned amount instead of the actual requirement. Loan amounts are often sanctioned generously to cover contingencies — draw down only what's needed each semester to minimize interest accrual on undisbursed funds.

4. Not checking collateral and margin money requirements. Loans above a certain threshold (commonly ₹7.5 lakh) often require collateral or a co-applicant, and most lenders require margin money (a portion of costs to be self-funded) — plan for this upfront.

5. Overlooking employment/repayment risk after the course. Since regular EMIs may start before securing stable employment, keep a buffer or consider partial interest servicing during the course to avoid repayment stress.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a moratorium period in an education loan? +

The moratorium period is the course duration plus a grace period (usually 6-12 months after course completion or securing a job, whichever is earlier) during which the borrower is not required to pay EMIs, though interest typically continues to accrue.

Does interest accrue during the moratorium period? +

Yes, for most education loans, simple interest accrues on the disbursed amount during the moratorium period. This accrued interest is usually capitalized (added to the principal) at the end of the moratorium, and EMI is then calculated on this higher principal.

Is it beneficial to pay interest during the moratorium period? +

Yes, paying at least the accruing interest during the moratorium (called 'simple interest servicing') prevents it from being capitalized into the principal, which reduces your total EMI and total interest once repayment begins — many lenders also offer a small interest rate concession for this.

What is the tax benefit on education loan interest? +

Under Section 80E of the Income Tax Act, the entire interest paid on an education loan is deductible from taxable income, with no upper limit, for up to 8 years from the start of repayment. This deduction applies only to interest, not principal.

What is the maximum repayment tenure for an education loan? +

Education loan repayment tenure in India typically ranges from 5 to 15 years after the moratorium period ends, depending on the loan amount and lender policy.
