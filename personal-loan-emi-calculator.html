Understanding Personal Loan EMI

Created by Umasankar Maity — B.Tech in Electrical Engineering, with 11+ years of industrial experience.

Reviewed by the ElectroMechCalc editorial team, comprising a qualified electrical engineer, a mechanical engineer, and a Chartered Accountant.

Last reviewed: July 2026  |  Standards referenced: RBI lending conventions / standard amortization formula

A personal loan is an unsecured loan — meaning no collateral like property or gold is pledged against it — used for anything from medical expenses to weddings to debt consolidation. Because the lender takes on more risk without collateral, personal loans carry higher interest rates than secured loans, typically 10-24% p.a. depending on your credit score, income, and existing obligations.

Formula used: EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where P is the loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the tenure in months. This is the standard reducing-balance amortization formula used by banks and NBFCs across India.

Worked example: a ₹5,00,000 personal loan at 13% p.a. for 3 years (36 months) gives a monthly rate of about 1.083%. Applying the formula gives an EMI of roughly ₹16,850. Over 36 months that's a total payment of about ₹6,06,600, of which around ₹1,06,600 is interest.

What lenders check before approval: personal loan eligibility depends heavily on your CIBIL score, monthly income, existing EMI obligations (FOIR), and employment stability, since there's no asset to fall back on if you default. A strong credit profile typically unlocks a meaningfully lower interest rate.

This calculator is for educational planning purposes only. Actual EMI may vary slightly depending on your lender's exact rounding and processing conventions; confirm the precise figure with your bank or NBFC.

Saving on Interest

How Tenure Changes Total Interest on a Personal Loan

Stretching a personal loan's tenure lowers the EMI but increases total interest paid, since interest accrues for longer on the outstanding balance — the effect is sharper here than on secured loans because personal loan rates are already higher. Since most lenders cap personal loan tenure at 5 years, the tradeoff window is narrower, but choosing the shortest tenure your monthly budget can comfortably absorb still saves meaningfully on lifetime interest cost.

Alternatives

Personal Loan vs Credit Card vs Loan Against Assets

Option Typical Rate (p.a.) Best for
Personal loan10-24%Lump-sum, fixed-tenure needs
Credit card revolving balance30-45%Short-term, small amounts only
Loan against fixed depositFD rate + 1-2%If you hold an FD and don't want to break it
Gold loan9-18%Quick disbursal, own gold jewellery

A personal loan usually beats carrying a revolving credit card balance by a wide margin, since card interest is charged daily on outstanding dues and compounds faster in practice. If you hold a fixed deposit or gold jewellery, a loan against those assets is often cheaper than an unsecured personal loan, since the asset itself reduces the lender's risk — the trade-off is a lower loan amount and the asset being lien-marked or pledged until repayment.

Balance transfer consideration: if your existing personal loan carries a high rate and your credit profile has improved since you took it, transferring the balance to a new lender at a lower rate can reduce your EMI — but always net the transfer/processing fee against the interest you'd actually save over the remaining tenure before switching.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes When Taking a Personal Loan

1. Borrowing more than needed "just in case." Since personal loans carry high interest, every extra rupee borrowed adds avoidable interest cost — borrow only what the actual expense requires.

2. Ignoring the processing fee and other charges. Processing fees (often 1-3% of the loan amount) plus GST and other charges add to your effective borrowing cost beyond what the EMI figure shows — always ask for the all-in cost before signing.

3. Not comparing the effective annual rate across lenders. Advertised rates can hide processing fees and insurance add-ons — compare the effective annual percentage rate (APR), not just the headline interest rate.

4. Taking a new personal loan to repay an existing one without checking the real savings. A balance transfer only helps if the new effective rate, minus transfer charges, is meaningfully lower than what you're currently paying.

5. Overlooking the impact on your credit utilization and FOIR. A large personal loan increases your fixed obligation to income ratio, which can hurt your eligibility for future loans like a home loan — plan your borrowing with future needs in mind.

6. Choosing a lender by advertised rate alone, ignoring the FOIR cap. Even at an attractive rate, lenders typically cap total EMI obligations (including the new loan) at 40-50% of monthly income — a loan that looks affordable on paper can still get rejected or reduced in amount for this reason.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How is personal loan EMI calculated? +

Personal loan EMI is calculated using the reducing-balance formula EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the tenure in months.

Why is the interest rate on a personal loan higher than a home loan? +

Personal loans are unsecured — there's no collateral backing the loan — so lenders charge a higher interest rate (typically 10-24% p.a.) to compensate for the higher risk compared to secured loans like home or car loans.

Does my credit score affect my personal loan EMI? +

Yes, a higher CIBIL score (750+) typically qualifies you for a lower interest rate, which directly reduces your EMI and total interest paid. Lenders view a low score as higher risk and price the loan accordingly.

Can I prepay or foreclose a personal loan early? +

Most lenders allow foreclosure after a lock-in period (often 12 months), usually with a prepayment charge of 2-5% on the outstanding principal. Check your loan agreement, as RBI rules exempt floating-rate loans to individual borrowers from foreclosure charges at many banks.

What is the maximum tenure for a personal loan? +

Personal loan tenures in India typically range from 1 to 5 years, shorter than secured loans, since lenders want to limit exposure on an unsecured product.

Is a personal loan cheaper than a credit card balance? +

Almost always yes — credit card revolving interest typically runs 30-45% p.a., roughly double even an expensive personal loan. If you're carrying a card balance month to month, a personal loan to pay it off and close it out usually reduces your total interest cost significantly.

What documents are typically required for a personal loan? +

Most lenders ask for identity and address proof (PAN, Aadhaar), income proof (salary slips or ITR for the self-employed), bank statements for the last 3-6 months, and passport-size photographs. Pre-approved offers for existing customers with a strong repayment history may need minimal additional documentation.

Can I get a personal loan with a low or no credit score? +

It's harder and more expensive — lenders either reject low-score applicants or approve them at a noticeably higher interest rate to offset the risk. Some NBFCs and fintech lenders cater specifically to thin-file or lower-score borrowers, but usually at a materially higher cost than a bank would charge a prime borrower.
