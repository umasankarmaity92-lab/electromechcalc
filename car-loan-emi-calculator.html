How it works

Understanding Car Loan EMI

A car loan finances the purchase of a vehicle, with the loan amount typically being the on-road price minus your down payment. Lenders in India usually finance 80-90% of the on-road price, and repayment happens through fixed monthly EMIs over a tenure of 1 to 7 years.

Formula used: Loan Amount = On-Road Price − Down Payment. EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the tenure in months.

Worked example: a car with an on-road price of ₹10,00,000 and a down payment of ₹2,00,000 gives a loan amount of ₹8,00,000. At 9% p.a. for 5 years (60 months), the monthly rate is 0.75%, giving an EMI of roughly ₹16,606. Over 60 months that's a total payment of about ₹9,96,360, of which around ₹1,96,360 is interest.

Secured loan advantage: since the car itself serves as collateral (the lender holds the hypothecation until the loan is repaid), car loan interest rates are lower than personal loans, typically 8-13% p.a. depending on whether the vehicle is new or used.

This calculator is for educational planning purposes only. Actual EMI may vary slightly depending on your lender's exact rounding and processing conventions; confirm the precise figure with your bank or NBFC.

A larger down payment also improves your loan-to-value ratio, which can help you negotiate a better interest rate with some lenders.

Insurance & Paperwork

Insurance, Hypothecation, and Foreclosure

Comprehensive insurance is mandatory, not optional, on a financed car. As long as the lender holds a hypothecation on the vehicle, they require a comprehensive (not just third-party) insurance policy with the lender's name endorsed on it, since the car is the collateral securing your loan. Some lenders bundle a multi-year policy into the loan itself, which quietly raises the financed amount and the EMI — check whether insurance is being financed or paid separately before signing.

Hypothecation and the RC (Registration Certificate). Until the loan is closed, the vehicle's RC carries a hypothecation entry in the lender's favour, which restricts you from selling the car freely. On full repayment, you must obtain a Form 35 (hypothecation cancellation) from the lender and get the RC updated with the RTO — skipping this step is a common reason resale gets complicated years later.

Foreclosure and prepayment charges. Unlike home loans, car loan prepayment charges are not uniformly waived by RBI for individual borrowers — many lenders levy 2-6% of the outstanding principal as a foreclosure charge, especially within the first 12-24 months. Always check this clause before assuming a planned prepayment will be cost-free.

Loan against an existing car / balance transfer. If your current car loan carries a high rate, a balance transfer to another lender at a lower rate can reduce your EMI, but factor in the transfer processing fee and any foreclosure charge on the existing loan before deciding whether the switch is genuinely worthwhile over the remaining tenure.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes When Taking a Car Loan

1. Financing the full on-road price with zero down payment. A larger upfront down payment reduces the loan amount, EMI, and total interest — even a modest 10-20% down payment meaningfully lowers your borrowing cost.

2. Choosing the longest tenure to minimize the monthly EMI. Cars depreciate quickly, so stretching the loan tenure risks being "upside down" — owing more than the car is worth — for longer, in addition to paying more total interest.

3. Ignoring add-on costs bundled into the loan. Extended warranties, insurance premiums, and accessories are sometimes financed into the loan amount, quietly increasing your EMI — check the loan breakdown carefully.

4. Not comparing new-car vs used-car loan rates. Used car loans carry meaningfully higher interest rates than new car loans — factor this into your buy-new-vs-buy-used decision, not just the sticker price.

5. Skipping the total-cost comparison across lenders. Dealership-arranged financing isn't always the cheapest option — compare the effective rate and processing fees from your bank or NBFC before signing at the showroom.

6. Not checking foreclosure charges before planning a prepayment. Car loans, unlike floating-rate home loans, often still carry a prepayment penalty — confirm the exact charge in your loan agreement so your "prepay early to save interest" plan doesn't get eaten up by a foreclosure fee.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How is car loan EMI calculated? +

Car loan EMI is calculated on the loan amount (on-road price minus down payment) using the reducing-balance formula EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the tenure in months.

How much down payment should I make for a car loan? +

Most lenders finance 80-90% of the on-road price, requiring a 10-20% down payment. A larger down payment reduces your loan amount, EMI, and total interest paid over the loan tenure.

What is the typical tenure for a car loan in India? +

Car loan tenures in India typically range from 1 to 7 years, with most borrowers choosing 3-5 years to balance a manageable EMI against total interest cost.

Does a new car loan have a lower interest rate than a used car loan? +

Yes, new car loans generally carry lower interest rates (around 8-10% p.a.) than used car loans (around 11-16% p.a.), since new vehicles hold collateral value better and carry lower default risk for the lender.

Can I claim tax benefits on a car loan? +

For personal use vehicles, car loan interest is generally not tax-deductible. However, if the vehicle is used for business purposes, the interest and depreciation may be claimed as a business expense — consult a tax professional for your specific situation.

Is comprehensive insurance mandatory for a financed car? +

Yes. As long as the lender holds hypothecation on the vehicle, a comprehensive insurance policy with the lender endorsed on it is mandatory, not optional — this protects the lender's collateral, not just you. Check whether the premium is financed into your loan or paid separately, since a financed premium raises your EMI.

Can I sell my car before the loan is fully repaid? +

Only with the lender's consent, since the RC carries a hypothecation entry until the loan closes. In practice this usually means the buyer's payment is used to foreclose the loan first, the lender issues a no-objection/hypothecation cancellation (Form 35), and only then is the RC transferred to the new owner.

Does foreclosing a car loan early always save money? +

Usually, but not always — most lenders charge a foreclosure fee of roughly 2-6% of the outstanding principal, especially if you foreclose within the first year or two. Work out the interest you'd save against this fee before assuming foreclosure is automatically the cheaper option.

Does a car loan affect my eligibility for a home loan later? +

Yes — an active car loan EMI counts toward your Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio (FOIR), which lenders use to assess how much additional EMI (like a future home loan) you can comfortably service. A large car loan EMI can reduce the home loan amount you're eligible for.
