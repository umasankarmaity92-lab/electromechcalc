How it works

How to Calculate Motor Full Load Current

Quick answer: three-phase motor FLC = kW × 1000 ÷ (1.732 × V × PF × η). Example: 15 kW at 415 V, PF 0.85, 90% efficiency ≈ 27.3 A.

Full Load Current (FLC), also called Full Load Amps (FLA), is the steady running current at rated output. It is the starting figure for cable, contactor and overload selection.

This tool works as a kW to amps, HP to amps and FLA calculator for 3-phase, single-phase and DC motors at any voltage up to 1000 V. For heaters and other non-motor loads, use the kW to Current Calculator.

Motor Current Formula

Three-phase motor (shaft kW)
I =kW × 1000√3 × V × PF × η
Single-phase motor (shaft kW)
I =kW × 1000V × PF × η
Known electrical input kW (3-phase)
I =kWin × 1000√3 × V × PF
DC motor
I =kW × 1000V × η
HP to watts
P (W) = HP × 746

I = current (A) · kW = rated shaft output · V = voltage (line-to-line for 3-phase) · PF = power factor · η = efficiency as a decimal.

Output vs input kW: nameplate kW/HP is mechanical output, so divide by η. If you already know the electrical input kW (from a meter), do not divide by η again — use the “Electrical input” option.

FLA vs FLC vs Rated Current

FLA — Full-Load AmpsThe current a motor draws at rated output, voltage and frequency. Usually the value printed on the nameplate.
FLC — Full-Load CurrentSame idea. In NEC work, FLC often means the standardised table value (Tables 430.248/430.250), which can differ from nameplate FLA.
Rated currentThe manufacturer's nameplate current at the stated voltage. Dual-voltage motors list one value per voltage.
SFA — Service-Factor AmpsCurrent at service-factor load (e.g. SF 1.15). Higher than FLA; not the normal running current.
Starting / locked-rotor current (LRA)The large current at standstill and during acceleration. Set by motor design (NEMA code letter / IEC IL/IN ratio), supply and starting method — not by FLA alone.
No-load currentMostly magnetising current when running unloaded. Varies widely by design and size.

Why FLC Drives Every Downstream Choice

Cable: needs enough ampacity for the installation method, ambient temperature, grouping and voltage drop — size it with the Cable Size Calculator, then check the run with the Voltage Drop Calculator.

Contactor: rated for the motor current under AC-3 duty.

Overload relay: set from the nameplate current, relay class and code/manufacturer rules.

Short-circuit protection: chosen per code and must ride through starting current (often 5–8× FLC for DOL).

Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting efficiency. Using shaft kW as input power understates FLC by 10% or more.
  • Dividing by efficiency twice. If your kW is already electrical input, don't apply η again.
  • One PF for every size. Small and lightly loaded motors run at lower PF.
  • Missing or wrongly adding √3. The error is a factor of about 1.73.
  • Treating a fixed % as code sizing. Allowances depend on local code and starter type.

NEC FLC Table vs Actual Motor FLA

Under the US NEC, conductor and short-circuit protection sizing for most motors uses the standardised FLC tables, not the nameplate. Overload protection uses the nameplate FLA.

Table values are fixed per HP and voltage, so they differ from a formula result using a specific motor's PF and efficiency. Low-speed, high-torque and multispeed motors use nameplate values instead.

Finding the FLA of a Specific Motor Model

Catalog numbers (like those printed on NEMA-frame or DC motors) map to one manufacturer datasheet. A generic formula cannot give that exact value.

Read the nameplate or the manufacturer's datasheet at the connected voltage. Note FLA, SFA, service factor, code letter, efficiency and PF — then use this calculator only to cross-check.

MCA vs FLA

MCA (Minimum Circuit Ampacity) on HVAC and packaged equipment is a code-derived conductor figure. It typically includes 125% of the largest motor plus other loads, so it cannot be converted back to one motor's FLA. Use the FLA/RLA values on the same nameplate.

Starting Methods

How Starting Method Affects Inrush Current

FLC is the running current. At start, an induction motor briefly draws far more — how much depends on motor design, supply strength and starting method.

Starting Method Typical Inrush Starting Torque
Direct-On-Line (DOL)5–8× FLAFull (100%)
Star-DeltaTypically ~2–3× FLA at the supply, depending on motor and system conditions~33% of DOL
Auto-transformer1.7–4× FLA (tap-dependent)Tap-dependent
Soft starterCommonly configurable ~2–4× FLA, depending on current-limit setting, motor and loadAdjustable, smooth ramp
VFD (Variable Frequency Drive)Typically ~1–1.5× FLA with properly configured drive controlDepends on VFD control mode, motor, and load

DOL is simplest and cheapest but gives the highest inrush and mechanical shock — fine for small motors on a strong supply.

Star-delta, soft starters and VFDs reduce inrush, protect belts and couplings, and limit voltage dips for larger motors or weak supplies.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Content last reviewed: September 2026

How do you calculate motor full load current (FLA)? +

Three-phase: I = (kW × 1000) ÷ (1.732 × V × PF × η). Single-phase: I = (kW × 1000) ÷ (V × PF × η). Here kW is the rated shaft output and η is efficiency as a decimal. Example: 15 kW at 415 V, PF 0.85, 90% efficiency ≈ 27.3 A. For HP, multiply by 746 to get watts first.

Should I use the calculated current or the motor's nameplate FLC? +

Prefer the nameplate FLC/FLA whenever it is available — it reflects that motor's tested design. Use the calculated value when the nameplate is missing, to cross-check a suspicious value, or before a motor model is chosen.

Should I enter the motor's output kW or input kW? +

Nameplate kW or HP is mechanical shaft output, so divide by efficiency. If you already know the electrical input kW (for example from a power meter), choose "Electrical input" in the calculator — efficiency must not be applied a second time.

What is the full-load current of a 3-phase motor? +

It depends on voltage, power, efficiency and PF. At PF 0.85 and 90% efficiency, a 3-phase motor draws roughly 1.8-1.9 A per kW at 400-415 V (real small motors draw more), and proportionally less at 460 V or 600 V. For example, 15 kW at 415 V ≈ 27.3 A; 10 HP at 460 V ≈ 12.2 A.

What is the full load current of a 50 HP motor? +

By formula (PF 0.85, 90% efficiency) a 50 HP (37.3 kW) motor draws about 67.8 A at 415 V and about 61.2 A at 460 V. For US code work, NEC Table 430.250 lists 65 A at 460 V and 130 A at 230 V for a 50 HP three-phase induction motor.

What is the difference between FLA and FLC? +

Both mean full-load current. FLA (full-load amps) usually refers to the motor nameplate value. In NEC work, FLC often means the standardised value from Table 430.248/430.250, which is used for conductor and short-circuit protection sizing, while the nameplate FLA is used for overload protection.

Why does a three-phase motor draw less current than a single-phase motor of the same power? +

The √3 factor means three-phase power is shared across three line conductors. The same power therefore needs less current per line, allowing smaller cable and switchgear.

Why is efficiency used to calculate current, since current relates to input power? +

Nameplate power is mechanical output. Losses in the windings, core and bearings make electrical input higher than output, so dividing by efficiency converts rated output to the real electrical input used in the current formula.

Why does a motor draw so much more current when starting than when running? +

At standstill the rotor acts like a short-circuited transformer winding, so impedance is very low until the motor builds speed. DOL starting current is typically 5-8× FLC, depending on motor design and supply — which is why reduced-voltage starters, soft starters and VFDs exist.

Should I size the breaker for FLA or for starting inrush current? +

Neither alone. Overload protection is set close to nameplate FLA. Short-circuit/ground-fault protection is selected per the applicable code and must tolerate the permitted starting current without nuisance tripping. Exact devices and settings depend on local code and starting method.

What is the no-load current of an induction motor? +

No-load current is mainly magnetising current. It varies widely with motor design and size — small motors often run at a much higher percentage of FLC on no load than large motors. Measure it or use manufacturer test data rather than a fixed rule.

Can I convert MCA to FLA? +

Not reliably. MCA (minimum circuit ampacity, common on HVAC nameplates) is a code-derived conductor figure that typically includes 125% of the largest motor plus other loads. Use the individual motor FLA or RLA values printed on the equipment nameplate instead.

How do I find the FLA of a specific motor model or catalog number? +

Check the motor nameplate or the manufacturer's datasheet for that catalog number, at the exact voltage the motor is connected to. Dual-voltage motors list a different FLA for each voltage, and some also list a service-factor amps (SFA) value. Do not rely on a generic estimate for a specific model.

Does power factor correction change the motor's FLC? +

Capacitors reduce the reactive current drawn upstream of the correction point, lowering line current and kVA seen by the supply. They do not change the current the motor itself draws at its terminals. Use the APFC Capacitor Calculator to size correction.

Can this calculator be used for DC motors? +

Yes — select DC. For a DC motor, I = (kW × 1000) ÷ (V × η), with no power factor or √3. Starting current of a DC motor depends on armature resistance and the starter used, so it is not estimated here.
