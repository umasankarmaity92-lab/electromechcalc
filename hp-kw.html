How it works

Understanding HP to kW Conversion

  • 1 HP = 0.746 kW and 1 kW ≈ 1.34 HP.
  • HP and kW both measure power, the rate of doing work.
  • HP: from James Watt. Common in the USA and on older or imported equipment.
  • kW: the SI unit. Standard on motors, generators and transformers worldwide.
  • The factor is fixed. No voltage, current or power factor is needed, unlike kVA.
  • kW = power. kWh = energy. Electricity bills are in kWh.

HP to kW Conversion Formula

kW = HP × 0.746
HP = kW ÷ 0.746

0.7457 vs 0.746

  • 0.7457 kW (745.7 W) is the more precise value of 1 mechanical HP.
  • 0.746 kW is the rounded value used in field work. This page uses it everywhere.
  • The difference is about 0.04%. Example: 100 HP = 74.57 kW (0.7457) vs 74.60 kW (0.746).
  • Error comes only from rounding and from choosing the wrong HP type.

How to Convert HP to kW (Step by Step)

  1. Check the HP type on the nameplate: mechanical, metric or boiler.
  2. Multiply HP by the factor for that type.
  3. For kW to HP, divide kW by the same factor.
Mechanical HP: kW = HP × 0.746
Metric HP (PS/CV): kW = HP × 0.7355
Boiler HP: kW = HP × 9.8095

Example 1: 15 HP pump

15 × 0.746 = 11.19 kW

Example 2: 25 kW generator

25 ÷ 0.746 = 33.51 HP
  • Used for: imported pumps vs local kW motors, nameplate cross-checks, SI spec sheets.
  • The result is a unit conversion only, not a motor selection.

Shaft Power vs Electrical Input Power

  • Nameplate HP or kW = shaft output power, not power drawn from the supply.
  • Input is higher because of motor losses (heat, friction, magnetic).
Input kW = Shaft kW ÷ η
1-phase: I = (kW × 1000) ÷ (η × V × PF)
3-phase: I = (kW × 1000) ÷ (√3 × η × V × PF)

Assumptions

  • kW = shaft (nameplate) output power.
  • η = efficiency as a decimal, at the operating load. Take it from the nameplate or datasheet.
  • PF = power factor at the operating load, also from the datasheet.
  • V = supply voltage (line-to-line for three-phase). Three-phase load is balanced.
  • Result = running current, not starting current.
  • Skipping η or √3 gives undersized cables and breakers.
  • Use the kW to Current calculator or Motor Current calculator. For starting current, see Motor Starting Current.

Common HP to kW Conversion Mistakes

  • Shaft power taken as input power. Mains power is higher by the motor losses.
  • Mechanical and metric HP mixed up. 0.746 vs 0.7355 kW per HP.
  • Motor chosen from the converted value alone. Check torque, duty, speed, starting and the manufacturer table.
  • Duty cycle and ambient temperature ignored. Hot or intermittent duty needs extra margin.
  • 3-phase treated as a different conversion. It is not. Only current changes (see the FAQ).

Motor Efficiency: Shaft kW vs Input kW

Efficiency (η) = Shaft kW ÷ Input kW
  • Example (assumed 90%): 10 kW at the shaft draws about 11.1 kW. About 1.1 kW is lost as heat.
  • Efficiency is not a fixed number. It depends on motor size, design, efficiency class and load.
  • IE classes (IE1 to IE4, IEC 60034-30-1) rank motors by efficiency. Higher class = lower losses.
  • Always take η from the nameplate or datasheet. Examples on this page use assumed values.
  • It sets the real supply demand, so it sets cable and breaker size.
  • Lower efficiency means higher running cost for the same work.

  • Boiler HP rates steam output, not shaft power. 1 boiler HP ≈ 9.8095 kW, over 13× a mechanical HP.
  • "BHP" can mean brake or boiler horsepower. Confirm before converting.

HP vs BHP vs kW: Key Differences

  • HP: unit of power. 1 HP = 0.746 kW.
  • kW: SI unit of power.
  • BHP (brake horsepower): not a new unit. Power measured at the shaft with a brake dynamometer.
  • Motor nameplate HP or kW is normally already shaft (brake) power. BHP and nameplate kW mean the same thing.
  • In a steam context, BHP can mean boiler horsepower.

IEC vs NEMA Motor Ratings and Applications

  • IEC (Europe, Asia, India): rated in kW. Steps: 0.75, 1.1, 1.5, 2.2, 3, 4, 5.5, 7.5, 11, 15, 18.5, 22, 30 kW.
  • NEMA (North America): rated in HP. Steps: 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 5, 7.5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 40, 50 HP.
  • Frame, shaft and mounting dimensions differ. Frame size varies by manufacturer, so use the catalogue.
  • Frequency: 50 Hz is common with IEC, 60 Hz with NEMA. A tendency, not a rule. Read the nameplate.
  • To swap IEC and NEMA motors, check power, frame, shaft, speed and frequency.
FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How many kW is 1 HP, 5 HP, 7.5 HP, 10 HP, 15 HP or 20 HP? +

At 0.746 kW per mechanical HP: 1 HP = 0.746 kW, 5 HP = 3.73 kW, 7.5 HP = 5.60 kW, 10 HP = 7.46 kW, 15 HP = 11.19 kW and 20 HP = 14.92 kW. Motors of these sizes are commonly labelled 0.75, 3.7, 5.5, 7.5, 11 and 15 kW. Check the manufacturer's datasheet.

How do I convert HP to kW? +

Multiply HP by 0.746: kW = HP × 0.746. Example: 15 HP × 0.746 = 11.19 kW. For metric HP use 0.7355, and for boiler HP use 9.8095.

How many HP is 1 kW? How do I convert kW to HP? +

Divide kW by 0.746: HP = kW ÷ 0.746. So 1 kW ≈ 1.34 HP, and 25 kW ≈ 33.51 HP.

Is 0.746 the exact HP to kW conversion factor? +

No. 0.746 is a rounded factor. The more precise value is 0.7457 kW (745.7 W) per mechanical HP, a difference of about 0.04%. For 100 HP that is 74.57 kW vs 74.60 kW. This calculator and its tables use 0.746.

How accurate is an HP to kW conversion? +

Error comes only from rounding and from choosing the wrong HP type (mechanical, metric or boiler). The result is a unit conversion, not a motor-selection or safety calculation. Verify ratings against the manufacturer's datasheet.

Does HP to kW change for 3-phase motors? How do I find the current? +

No, the conversion is the same. Only current changes. Example: 10 HP at 415 V, assumed efficiency 0.90 and PF 0.85. Shaft power 7.46 kW, input 7.46 ÷ 0.90 = 8.29 kW, current = 8,290 ÷ (√3 × 415 × 0.85) ≈ 13.6 A. Use the nameplate efficiency and PF.

Does converting HP to kW give the motor's electrical input power? +

No. Nameplate HP or kW is mechanical shaft output. Input power = shaft kW ÷ efficiency. Current then depends on voltage, power factor and phase (see the formulas above).

What is the difference between mechanical HP and metric HP? +

Mechanical HP = 745.7 W (0.746 kW). Metric HP (PS/CV) = 735.5 W (0.7355 kW), about 1.4% smaller. It mainly matters for older European or Japanese equipment.

Is boiler horsepower the same as mechanical horsepower? +

No. 1 boiler HP ≈ 9.8095 kW, over 13 times a mechanical HP, and it rates steam output, not shaft power. Never use 0.746 on a boiler rating.

Why does a motor nameplate kW not match the calculated HP conversion? +

Motors are sold in catalogue steps, and the HP label is a rounded convention. For example, 5 HP calculates to 3.73 kW but is commonly labelled 3.7 kW. A calculated value that is not a catalogue size is not a motor rating.
