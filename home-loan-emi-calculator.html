Understanding Home Loan EMI

Created by Umasankar Maity — B.Tech in Electrical Engineering, with 11+ years of industrial experience.

Reviewed by the ElectroMechCalc editorial team, comprising a qualified electrical engineer, a mechanical engineer, and a Chartered Accountant.

Last reviewed: July 2026  |  Standards referenced: RBI lending conventions / Income Tax Act (80C, 24b)

A home loan EMI follows the same reducing-balance formula as any other term loan, but home loans typically come with the longest tenures (up to 30 years) and lowest interest rates of any retail loan category, since the property itself serves as collateral. This calculator estimates your monthly EMI, total interest, and year-wise amortization for any home loan amount, rate, and tenure.

Formula used: EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where P is the loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the tenure in months.

Worked example: a ₹30,00,000 home loan at 8.5% p.a. for 20 years (240 months) gives a monthly rate of 0.708%. The EMI works out to roughly ₹26,035, with total interest of about ₹32,48,400 over the full tenure — more than the original principal, which is typical for a long-tenure home loan.

Tax benefits on home loan EMI: under the Old Tax Regime, the principal component of your EMI qualifies for a deduction under Section 80C (up to ₹1.5 lakh, combined with other 80C investments), while the interest component qualifies for a deduction under Section 24(b) — up to ₹2 lakh per year for a self-occupied property. This calculator's year-wise breakdown shows the principal/interest split, which is exactly the split relevant for claiming these deductions.

Loan-to-Value (LTV): most lenders finance up to 75-90% of the property value depending on the loan amount, meaning you'll need a down payment covering the remaining 10-25% plus registration and other charges — factor this into your total home-buying budget, not just the EMI affordability.

This calculator is for educational planning purposes only. Actual EMI, eligible loan amount, and tax benefits depend on your specific lender's terms and your personal tax situation; confirm with your bank and a tax advisor.

Extending a home loan from 20 to 30 years reduces the EMI by roughly ₹3,000/month but adds over ₹20 lakh in total interest — the classic long-tenure trade-off, magnified by the large principal typical of home loans. Since Section 24(b)'s ₹2 lakh interest deduction cap is fixed regardless of your actual interest paid, borrowers in higher tax brackets with large loans often find that beyond a certain point, additional interest isn't fully tax-shielded — worth factoring in when deciding between prepaying and investing surplus funds elsewhere.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes When Planning a Home Loan

1. Maximizing loan tenure just to lower EMI. As the table above shows, a 30-year tenure can add over ₹20 lakh in interest compared to 20 years for the same loan — choose the shortest tenure your budget can comfortably sustain.

2. Forgetting the down payment and other upfront costs. Beyond the 10-25% down payment, budget for registration, stamp duty, processing fees, and legal charges — these can add 7-10% on top of the property price.

3. Not claiming both 80C and 24(b) deductions. Many borrowers only claim the interest deduction and forget the principal qualifies separately under 80C (subject to the overall ₹1.5 lakh 80C cap) — check both, especially under the Old Tax Regime.

4. Ignoring floating-rate risk on a 20-30 year loan. Over a multi-decade tenure, interest rates will almost certainly move — understand whether your EMI or your tenure adjusts when the lender's rate changes, and budget for some rate flexibility.

5. Not comparing the total cost across lenders, only the headline rate. Processing fees, prepayment charges, and other terms vary between lenders even at similar headline rates — compare total cost of the loan, not just the advertised interest rate.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How is home loan EMI calculated? +

Home loan EMI follows the standard reducing-balance formula EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where P is the sanctioned loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the tenure in months. Because home loan tenures run up to 30 years, even a small rate difference has an outsized effect on lifetime interest compared to shorter loans.

Why does a home loan carry the lowest interest rate among retail loans? +

The property itself is mortgaged to the lender as collateral until the loan is fully repaid, which sharply lowers the lender's risk compared to an unsecured product like a personal loan. That lower risk, combined with the priority-sector and long-tenure nature of housing finance, is why home loan rates sit well below most other retail loan categories.

How does a floating home loan rate change over a 20-30 year tenure? +

Most floating-rate home loans today are linked to an External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR) — usually the RBI repo rate plus a lender-specific spread. When the RBI revises the repo rate, lenders are required to pass the change through within a set period, which then adjusts either your EMI or your remaining tenure, depending on the lender's policy and any standing instruction you've given.

Does prepaying a home loan actually save meaningful money? +

Yes, and the saving is proportionally larger on home loans than on shorter loans precisely because the tenure is so long — a lump-sum prepayment in year 3 of a 20-year loan removes nearly two decades of future interest on that principal. RBI rules also bar lenders from charging a foreclosure or prepayment penalty on floating-rate home loans taken by individual borrowers.

What happens if I miss a home loan EMI payment? +

A missed home loan EMI attracts a late payment fee and dents your credit score, and the account is typically classified as a Special Mention Account (SMA) if payments stay overdue. Continued default for 90 days can see the loan classified as a Non-Performing Asset (NPA), after which the lender can initiate recovery proceedings against the mortgaged property under the SARFAESI Act.

Fixed or floating rate — which suits a long home loan tenure better? +

Very few Indian lenders now offer a truly fixed rate for the entire 20-30 year home loan tenure — most "fixed" options are actually fixed for an initial 2-5 years before reverting to floating. Given that a home loan runs for decades, most borrowers end up on a floating/EBLR-linked rate and instead manage rate risk through periodic prepayment rather than rate-locking.

Should I stretch my home loan to 30 years to keep the EMI low? +

A 30-year tenure keeps the EMI affordable but, as the comparison table above shows, can add tens of lakhs in extra interest versus a 20-year tenure on the same loan. A common middle path is taking the longer tenure for eligibility and cash-flow comfort, then prepaying opportunistically each year to cut the effective payoff period.

What tax benefits can I claim on my home loan EMI? +

Under the Old Tax Regime, the principal component of your EMI qualifies under Section 80C (up to ₹1.5 lakh combined with other 80C investments), and the interest component qualifies under Section 24(b) (up to ₹2 lakh for a self-occupied property). This calculator's amortization table shows the exact principal/interest split needed to claim these.

How much down payment do I need for a home loan? +

Lenders typically finance 75-90% of the property value (Loan-to-Value or LTV), meaning you need to arrange 10-25% as a down payment, plus registration, stamp duty, and processing charges on top — budget for these upfront costs separately from the EMI itself.
