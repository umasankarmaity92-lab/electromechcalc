    Quick Notes

    EPF at a Glance (2026)

    • What it is: Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) is a compulsory retirement savings account for salaried employees, managed by EPFO under the Ministry of Labour & Employment.
    • Your share: 12% of your monthly Basic + DA, deducted from salary.
    • Employer's share: another 12% — split into 3.67% to your EPF account and 8.33% to the Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS).
    • Interest rate: 8.25% p.a. for FY 2025-26, ratified by the government in June 2026 — the same rate for the third year in a row.
    • How interest works: calculated every month on the running balance, credited to your account once a year.
    • Wage ceiling: raised from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 per month — approved by the Union Cabinet on 16 September 2026 and effective from 17 September 2026 (PIB release).
    • One account for life: your UAN (Universal Account Number) stays the same across every job.
    • Legal framework: EPF now runs under the Code on Social Security, 2020 and the EPF Scheme, 2026, which replaced the old 1952 scheme.
    Step by Step

    How to Use This EPF Calculator

    1. Pick the input type — "Basic + DA" works out contributions from percentages; "Fixed Amount" takes your total monthly EPF credit (your share + employer EPF share, without EPS).
    2. Enter your monthly Basic + DA — take it from your latest payslip, not your CTC or gross salary.
    3. Enter your current age and projection age — the calculator projects your balance up to the age you choose. 58 is used as the default because it is the EPS pension age; full EPF withdrawal is allowed on retirement from 55.
    4. Check the contribution rates — 12% (you) and 3.67% (employer EPF) are the defaults for most employees.
    5. Add an annual step-up % — your expected yearly salary increment, e.g. 5–8%.
    6. Enter the interest rate and existing balance — use 8.25% and the current balance from your UAN passbook.
    7. Read the result — maturity value, the pie-chart split and the year-wise table update live as you type.
    Formula

    EPF Calculation Formula

    Monthly contribution = Basic + DA × (Employee % + Employer EPF %)

    Monthly interest = Opening balance × (Annual rate ÷ 12)

    Year-end balance = Opening balance + 12 months' contributions + year's interest

    Basic in year n = Basic × (1 + Step-up %)n−1

    Calculation method used: monthly interest is calculated on the opening balance, before that month's contribution is added, so a new contribution starts earning from the next month. The 12 months' interest is added to the balance at the end of each year. Your actual EPFO passbook can differ because of contribution dates, salary revisions, transfers, withdrawals and the interest rate declared for each financial year.

    EPF vs EPS

    Where Your Employer's 12% Actually Goes

    • 3.67% → EPF: added to your lump-sum corpus and earns interest — this is what the calculator projects.
    • 8.33% → EPS: funds a monthly pension after retirement; it does not earn interest in your account.
    • EPS is capped: it is calculated only on wages up to the ceiling — ₹25,000 from 17 September 2026 (earlier ₹15,000), so the maximum EPS share is about ₹2,083 per month (8.33% × ₹25,000). Source: Ministry of Labour & Employment via PIB. Employers are updating payroll, so confirm the change on your payslip.
    • Basic + DA above ₹25,000? If your employer contributes 12% on your full basic, the part above the EPS cap goes into EPF, so your employer EPF share is higher than 3.67%. Switch the employer field to ₹ and enter the actual amount from your passbook.
    • Pension eligibility: at least 10 years of service; full pension starts at 58, with a reduced early pension possible from 50.
    Withdrawal

    EPF Withdrawal Rules 2026

    • Partial withdrawal: allowed after 12 months of service, under three heads — essential needs (illness, education, marriage), housing, and special circumstances.
    • 25% minimum balance: while you are in service, at least 25% of your balance must stay in the account and keeps earning interest.
    • Full withdrawal: on retirement at 55 or later, permanent disability, retrenchment, voluntary retirement or permanently leaving India.
    • After losing a job: up to 75% can be withdrawn immediately; the remaining 25% after 12 months of unemployment.
    • EPS withdrawal: if you leave before 10 years of service, the pension amount can be withdrawn only after a 36-month wait (earlier 2 months).
    • Changing jobs? Transfer your balance through the UAN portal instead of withdrawing — compounding and your service record continue.
    • How to claim: online through the EPFO member portal or UMANG, with Aadhaar, PAN and bank KYC linked to your UAN.
    Tax

    Is EPF Taxable?

    • Your contribution: deductible only under the old tax regime, within the overall ₹1.5 lakh limit; no deduction under the new regime.
    • Interest: tax-free, except interest on your own contribution above ₹2.5 lakh a year (₹5 lakh where there is no employer contribution).
    • Employer contribution: taxable in your hands if the employer's EPF, NPS and superannuation contributions together exceed ₹7.5 lakh a year.
    • Withdrawal after 5 years of continuous service (including transferred service from earlier jobs): fully tax-free.
    • Withdrawal before 5 years: taxable; TDS of 10% applies above ₹50,000 (higher without PAN). Form 15G/15H can be submitted if your income is below the taxable limit.
    • Note: the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026 and renumbered most sections — always check the latest rules before filing.
    Avoid These

    Common EPF Planning Mistakes

    • Using CTC or gross salary instead of Basic + DA — this badly overstates the result.
    • Counting the full 24% as EPF — the employer's 8.33% goes to EPS, not your corpus.
    • Ignoring salary step-up — a flat-salary projection can understate maturity by ₹50 lakh or more.
    • Withdrawing on every job change — it breaks compounding and can trigger tax before 5 years.
    • Treating 8.25% as fixed for 30 years — the rate is declared every year; test a lower rate too.
    For Engineers

    EPF Tips for Engineers and Plant Staff

    • On contract or site postings: check your passbook every few months — a PF deduction on the payslip must show up as a deposit in the passbook.
    • Several UANs from past jobs? Merge them under "One Member – One EPF Account" so your service is counted together.
    • Overtime and allowances usually don't attract PF — contributions are on Basic + DA only.
    • Want more in EPF? Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF) lets you contribute above 12% at the same interest rate.
    • Keep KYC and nominee updated — Aadhaar, PAN and bank details linked to your UAN make claims much faster.
    • Check your gratuity too: after 5 years of service you are also entitled to gratuity — estimate it with the Gratuity Calculator.
    • EPF is a safe base, not the whole plan — most people need other long-term savings alongside it (general information, not financial advice).

    FAQ

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Is this EPF calculator free to use? +

    Yes. It is completely free, needs no sign-up, and the calculation runs entirely in your browser, so your salary details are not sent anywhere.

    How many times can I use the EPF calculator? +

    As many times as you like. Change any input and the result, pie chart and year-wise table update instantly, which makes it easy to compare scenarios.

    What is the current EPF interest rate? +

    8.25% per year for FY 2025-26. EPFO's Central Board of Trustees recommended it in March 2026 and the government ratified it in June 2026. It is the same rate as the previous two years.

    How accurate is this EPF calculation? +

    It follows EPFO's method: interest is calculated monthly on the opening balance and credited once a year. Your actual passbook can still differ because the interest rate is declared every year and real salary increases are rarely a flat percentage.

    Can I use the calculator after changing my job? +

    Yes. Your UAN stays the same, so transfer your old balance to the new employer, then enter the combined balance as the existing EPF balance and use your new Basic + DA.

    What if the interest rate or contribution changes later? +

    Simply re-run the calculator with the new values. For long-term planning, it is sensible to also test a slightly lower rate such as 7.5–8% to see a conservative outcome.

    Does my employer's full 12% go into my EPF account? +

    No. The employer's 12% is split into 3.67% for EPF and 8.33% for the Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS). Only the EPF part builds your lump-sum corpus; EPS pays a monthly pension after retirement.

    What is the EPF wage ceiling in 2026? +

    The Union Cabinet approved raising the mandatory wage ceiling from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 per month on 16 September 2026, effective from 17 September 2026 (PIB release). This increases EPF and EPS contributions for employees whose Basic + DA is between ₹15,000 and ₹25,000.

    When can I withdraw money from my EPF account? +

    Partial withdrawal is allowed after 12 months of service for essential needs, housing or special circumstances, while keeping at least 25% of the balance in the account. Full withdrawal is allowed on retirement at 55 or later and in cases such as permanent disability, retrenchment or permanently leaving India.

    Is EPF withdrawal taxable? +

    Withdrawal after 5 years of continuous service is tax-free. Before 5 years it is taxable, and 10% TDS applies on amounts above ₹50,000 unless you submit Form 15G/15H and your income is below the taxable limit.
