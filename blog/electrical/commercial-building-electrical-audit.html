1. Introduction

A commercial building electrical audit is a structured inspection and measurement exercise that reviews a building's electrical installation for energy efficiency, power quality and safety compliance. Unlike a one-time site visit, a proper audit combines instrument-based measurement over a representative period with a documented walk-through survey and a review of existing single-line diagrams, load schedules and maintenance history, ending in a written report with prioritised, costed recommendations.

This guide covers the full audit process — from scoping and pre-audit preparation through load analysis, power quality assessment, thermography, safety inspection and the standard report format used to communicate findings to building owners and facility managers.

2. Types of Electrical Audits

Energy audit: focused on consumption patterns, load factor, power factor and identifying energy-saving opportunities such as lighting retrofit, HVAC efficiency and power factor correction.

Safety audit: focused on compliance with electrical safety codes, earthing integrity, protective device coordination and physical hazard identification.

Combined audit: most commercial engagements combine both scopes into a single audit, since the underlying measurement work (load logging, thermography, panel inspection) overlaps significantly between the two.

3. Audit Scope & Pre-Audit Preparation

Before site work begins, the audit team should collect existing documentation and agree the scope boundary (which panels, floors and systems are included) with the building owner. Missing documentation is itself a common audit finding.

  • Collect single-line diagrams, load schedules and past maintenance records
  • Collect 12 months of electricity bills for baseline consumption and tariff structure review
  • Confirm sanctioned/contracted demand and current tariff category
  • Identify critical loads, standby power systems and any existing power quality complaints
  • Agree the measurement period (commonly 3-7 days including at least one representative peak day)
  • Confirm access, safety permits and any shutdown windows required for panel-internal inspection

4. Load Analysis & Demand Study

Load Factor = Average Demand ÷ Peak Demand

Load logging at the main incomer and key sub-distribution boards records current, voltage, power factor and demand (kW/kVA) over the measurement period. Key checks include the connected-load versus actual-demand ratio, phase balance across the three phases, and comparing recorded maximum demand against the sanctioned/contracted demand to flag any risk of demand-related penalty charges from the utility.

A low load factor (well below 0.5-0.6) often indicates oversized equipment or highly intermittent operation, both of which are worth investigating further for right-sizing or scheduling opportunities.

5. Power Quality Assessment

Total harmonic distortion (THD): measured on both voltage and current at the incomer and major non-linear-load feeders (VFDs, UPS, LED drivers); elevated current THD can overheat transformers and neutral conductors.

Power factor: measured at the incomer and key feeders; low power factor risks penalty charges under most Indian commercial tariff structures and indicates APFC capacity may need review.

Voltage unbalance: measured across the three phases; unbalance above roughly 2% accelerates motor heating and derates three-phase equipment.

Transients, sags and swells: logged over the measurement period to correlate with any reported equipment trips or nuisance resets.

6. Thermography Inspection

Infrared thermal imaging is performed on energised panels under normal load, without needing a shutdown, to detect abnormal heating at connections, breakers, cable terminations and busbar joints. A loose or corroded connection typically shows a localised hotspot well before it fails outright, making thermography one of the highest-value, lowest-disruption tools in a commercial electrical audit.

Findings are usually classified by temperature rise above ambient or above a reference (unaffected) component: minor (advisory), major (schedule repair soon), and critical (repair immediately / potential fire risk).

7. Earthing & Safety Inspection

  • Earth electrode resistance measurement per IS 3043
  • Earth continuity check on exposed metal enclosures and equipment bodies
  • RCD/ELCB functional (trip-time) testing
  • Panel labelling accuracy and single-line diagram verification
  • Fire-rated cable and cable tray condition in critical escape routes
  • Availability and validity of statutory test certificates
  • Danger/caution signage presence and condition
  • Physical condition of enclosures, cable glands and terminations for exposed live parts or moisture ingress risk

8. Panel & Protection Review

Protection coordination is checked by comparing breaker/fuse ratings and settings against the load schedule and short-circuit study (where available), verifying selective tripping between upstream and downstream devices, and confirming spare capacity for future load growth. Panel internals are visually inspected for signs of overheating, corrosion, loose busbar connections and correct torque on terminations, cross-referenced against the thermography findings from Section 6.

9. Lighting & HVAC Energy Review

  • Existing fixture inventory, wattage and operating hours versus current LED alternatives
  • Measured lux levels versus IS 3646/NLC targets by area — see this site's dedicated Energy-Efficient Industrial Lighting Guide for retrofit planning detail
  • HVAC chiller/AHU loading versus rated capacity and controls sequencing
  • Presence and effectiveness of occupancy, daylight and scheduling controls

10. Statutory Compliance Checks

  • Central Electricity Authority (Measures Relating to Safety and Electric Supply) Regulations, 2010 (as amended) compliance
  • State electrical inspectorate periodic inspection certificate validity, per the specific state's electrical inspectorate rules
  • Fire NOC electrical compliance items (where applicable to the jurisdiction)
  • Transformer/HT installation statutory testing (IR, oil BDV) where an on-site substation exists
  • Lift, DG set and fire pump electrical interlock verification

Statutory requirements are jurisdiction-specific and are periodically amended. This section names the relevant regulatory frameworks; it is not a substitute for checking the current, locally applicable edition with the relevant state electrical inspectorate or a licensed compliance professional before relying on it for an actual filing.

11. Instruments Used in an Audit

  • Power quality analyzer / data logger — for load, power factor, harmonics and voltage event logging
  • Thermal imaging camera — for connection and joint hotspot detection
  • Earth resistance tester — for electrode and continuity measurement
  • Insulation resistance (megger) tester — for cable and winding IR checks
  • Clamp meter — for spot current/voltage verification
  • Lux meter — for illuminance measurement against design targets
  • RCD/ELCB trip-time tester — for protective device functional verification

12. Audit Report Format

  • Executive summary
  • Audit scope and methodology
  • Measured data: load profiles, power quality readings, thermography images
  • Findings, categorised by severity (critical / major / minor)
  • Prioritised recommendations with estimated cost and payback where applicable
  • Appendix: raw data, single-line diagrams, and referenced test certificates

13. Recommended Audit Frequency

A general energy and safety audit is commonly recommended every 3-5 years for a stable-occupancy commercial building, or immediately after any major renovation, tenant change, or significant load addition. Statutory electrical safety inspections, distinct from an energy audit, are often required annually or per local electrical inspectorate regulations, which vary by state/jurisdiction in India — always confirm the current requirement with the relevant local authority.

14. Electrical Integration Notes

An audit's findings routinely point back to the same subsystems covered elsewhere on this site: panel and protection issues found during Section 8 map directly onto the maintenance practices in the HT/LT Panel Maintenance Guide, and any standby DG set included in the audit scope should be cross-checked against the Diesel Generator Maintenance Guide's A/B/C/D check schedule to confirm its maintenance records align with what the audit observed on inspection.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a commercial building electrical audit? +

A commercial building electrical audit is a structured inspection and measurement exercise covering energy consumption patterns, load distribution, power quality, and electrical safety/compliance of a building's electrical installation. It combines a walk-through survey, instrument-based measurements (load logging, power quality analysis, thermography) and a review of single-line diagrams and maintenance records, concluding in a written report with findings and prioritised recommendations.

How often should a commercial building electrical audit be conducted? +

A general energy and safety audit is commonly recommended every 3-5 years for a stable-occupancy commercial building, or immediately after any major renovation, tenant change, or significant load addition. Statutory electrical safety inspections (distinct from an energy audit) are often required annually or per local electrical inspectorate regulations, which vary by state/jurisdiction in India.

What is thermography and why is it used in an electrical audit? +

Thermography (infrared thermal imaging) is used during an electrical audit to detect abnormal heating at connections, breakers, cable terminations and busbar joints while the system is energised and under normal load, without needing to shut down or open panels. A loose or corroded connection typically shows a localised hotspot well before it fails, making thermography one of the highest-value, lowest-disruption tools in a commercial electrical audit.

What is included in the load analysis portion of an electrical audit? +

Load analysis typically includes logging current, voltage, power factor and demand (kW/kVA) at the main incomer and key sub-distribution boards over a representative period (commonly 3-7 days including a peak-demand day), reviewing the connected-load versus actual-demand ratio, checking phase balance across the three phases, and comparing the recorded maximum demand against the sanctioned/contracted demand to flag any risk of demand-related penalty charges.

What power quality issues does an electrical audit typically check for? +

A power quality audit typically checks total harmonic distortion (THD) on voltage and current, power factor at the incomer and key feeders, voltage unbalance across phases, and transient/sag/swell events logged over the measurement period. Elevated THD is increasingly common in buildings with large VFD, LED driver and UPS loads and can cause overheating in transformers and neutral conductors if not addressed.

What safety items are checked during an electrical safety audit? +

A safety-focused electrical audit checks earthing/grounding system continuity and resistance value, RCD/ELCB functional testing, panel labelling and single-line diagram accuracy, fire-rated cable and cable tray condition in critical escape routes, availability and validity of statutory test certificates, presence and condition of danger/caution signage, and physical condition of enclosures, cable glands and terminations for exposed live parts or ingress risk.

What does an electrical audit report typically contain? +

A standard commercial electrical audit report contains an executive summary, the audit scope and methodology, measured data (load profiles, power quality readings, thermography images), a findings section categorised by severity (critical/major/minor), prioritised recommendations with estimated cost and payback where applicable, and an appendix with raw data, single-line diagrams and test certificates referenced during the audit.

What is the difference between an energy audit and a safety audit for a commercial building? +

An energy audit focuses on consumption patterns, load factor, power factor and identifying energy-saving opportunities (lighting retrofit, HVAC efficiency, power factor correction), with financial payback as the primary output metric. A safety audit focuses on compliance with electrical safety codes, earthing integrity, protective device coordination and physical hazard identification, with risk reduction and statutory compliance as the primary output. Many commercial audits combine both scopes into a single engagement since the underlying measurement work overlaps significantly.
