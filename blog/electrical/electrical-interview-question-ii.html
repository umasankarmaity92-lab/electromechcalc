Module 4 · Q41–Q50

Transformer & Power Distribution

Transformer principle, kVA rating, losses, oil testing, Buchholz relay, cooling and protection.

Q41

Explain a transformer.

+

Definition

A transformer transfers AC energy between circuits by electromagnetic induction, normally changing voltage and current while keeping frequency the same.

Formula

V1 / V2 ≈ N1 / N2 ≈ I2 / I1

Model interview answer

“I monitor transformer loading, oil and winding temperature, oil condition, bushings, cooling system and protection devices. Any abnormal temperature or oil condition is investigated before it develops into a major failure.”

Q42

Why are transformers rated in kVA instead of kW?

+

Definition

Transformer losses — and therefore heating — depend on voltage (core loss) and current (copper loss), not on the power factor of the load. The manufacturer also doesn't know what PF the load will have.

Formula

Three-phase kVA = √3 × V_L × I_L / 1000

Model interview answer

“A transformer supplies both active and reactive load, so its current and voltage determine thermal loading. That is why its capacity is expressed in kVA.”

Q43

What are transformer losses?

+

Formula

Maximum efficiency occurs where copper loss = core loss

Types

  • Core (iron) loss: hysteresis + eddy current — roughly constant whenever energised
  • Copper (I²R) loss — varies with the square of load current
  • Stray and dielectric losses — smaller

Model interview answer

“I monitor loading and temperature because copper loss rises with the square of current. Cooling performance becomes critical as loading increases.”

Q44

Why is transformer oil used?

+

Definition

Transformer oil insulates the windings and transfers heat from the core and windings to the tank and radiators.

Model interview answer

“I check oil level, leaks, temperature, moisture, dielectric strength and, for larger units, dissolved gas analysis (DGA) trends. Deteriorated or contaminated oil reduces insulation reliability.”

Q45

What is the BDV test?

+

Definition

The breakdown voltage test measures the dielectric strength of insulating oil by raising voltage across a standard electrode gap until it breaks down (commonly per IEC 60156 or ASTM D1816/D877).

Model interview answer

“Low BDV indicates moisture, particles or oil deterioration. I follow the applicable test standard and compare results with the acceptance values in the plant/OEM criteria or oil-maintenance standard (e.g. IEC 60422). Results from different methods (IEC 60156, ASTM D1816, ASTM D877) use different electrodes and gaps, so I don't compare them directly.”

Q46

What is the transformer IR test?

+

Definition

Insulation resistance is measured between HV–LV, HV–earth and LV–earth, as applicable, with a DC insulation tester.

Model interview answer

“I isolate and discharge the transformer, then connect the tester in the approved test configuration — earthing or guarding the windings not under test as that procedure specifies. I compare temperature-corrected values with previous records, because the trend is more meaningful than one isolated number. PI and dielectric absorption ratio help assess moisture.”

Q47

What is a Buchholz relay?

+

Definition

A Buchholz relay sits in the pipe between the main tank and conservator of conservator-type oil transformers, and detects gas accumulation and sudden oil surge caused by internal faults.

Model interview answer

“Slow gas accumulation gives an alarm, while a severe oil surge initiates a trip. After operation I collect and analyse the gas where possible and investigate the transformer before re-energisation.”

Q48

Explain conservator, breather, ONAN and ONAF.

+

Definition

The conservator accommodates oil expansion. The silica-gel breather dries air entering the conservator. ONAN = oil natural, air natural cooling; ONAF = oil natural, air forced (fans).

Model interview answer

“I inspect oil level, breather silica gel colour and cooling fans. For a transformer with separate ONAN and ONAF nameplate ratings, loss of the fans means loading must be reduced towards the ONAN rating, following the manufacturer's instructions.”

Q49

Why does a transformer overheat or hum abnormally?

+

Possible causes

  • Overload
  • Poor cooling (blocked radiators, failed fans)
  • High ambient temperature
  • Harmonics
  • Overvoltage / over-fluxing
  • Loose core clamping or connections
  • Internal fault

Model interview answer

“I compare load, temperature, voltage and cooling status with normal conditions. Abnormal humming together with temperature rise needs further investigation rather than assuming it is normal magnetostriction noise.”

Q50

What are common transformer protection systems?

+

Key points

  • Differential protection
  • Overcurrent and earth fault
  • Restricted earth fault (REF)
  • Buchholz
  • Oil and winding temperature
  • Pressure relief device
  • Surge arresters
  • Proper earthing

Model interview answer

“I treat transformer protection as a complete system. After a trip I identify which protection operated, collect relay records and investigate the actual fault before restoration.”

Module 5 · Q51–Q60

Panels, Cables & Earthing

CTs and PTs, MCC/PCC panels, thermography, cable selection, terminations, earthing and safe isolation.

Q51

What are a CT and a PT/VT?

+

Definition

A current transformer steps a high primary current down to a proportional small current (typically 1 A or 5 A) for meters and relays. A potential/voltage transformer steps high voltage down to a safe measurable level (commonly 110 V or 100 V class, 120 V in North America).

Key points

  • A CT primary is in series with the circuit.
  • A PT/VT is connected across (in parallel with) the voltage.
  • Metering CTs and protection CTs have different accuracy classes.

Model interview answer

“In a plant, CTs and PTs feed meters and protection relays. During maintenance I check ratio, polarity, insulation, burden, connections and secondary circuit condition.”

Q52

Why must a CT secondary never be left open?

+

Definition

With primary current flowing, an open CT secondary has no opposing ampere-turns, so the core saturates and a dangerously high voltage appears across the secondary terminals.

Risks

  • Shock hazard
  • Insulation breakdown
  • CT overheating/damage
  • Meter or relay damage

Model interview answer

“I never open a CT secondary while primary current is flowing. Before disconnecting a CT-connected meter or relay, I use the approved CT shorting/test-block arrangement and follow the site safety procedure. If a CT secondary is found open on a live circuit, I don't try to handle or bridge it by hand — the circuit is made safe under the approved procedure first.”

Q53

What are MCC and PCC panels?

+

Definition

An MCC (motor control centre) controls and protects motors; a PCC (power control centre) distributes power to downstream boards and large loads.

Key components

  • Incomer
  • Busbars
  • MCCB/ACB
  • Contactors
  • Overload relays
  • CTs and meters
  • Control circuits
  • Earthing

Model interview answer

“In an MCC I focus on motor feeders, contactors, overloads, protection and control circuits. In a PCC I focus on power distribution, incomers, busbars, feeder protection and load management.”

Q54

How do you perform electrical panel maintenance?

+

Definition

Panel maintenance covers inspection, cleaning, tightening, testing and condition assessment of the electrical components.

Checkpoints

  • Loose terminals
  • Busbar condition
  • Dust and moisture
  • Hotspots
  • Breakers and contactors
  • Earthing
  • Insulation
  • Cable terminations

Model interview answer

“I isolate the panel under LOTO and verify the dead condition. Then I inspect busbars, breakers, contactors, terminals, cables, insulation and earthing. I carry out torque checks to the manufacturer's values and thermography where applicable, and record abnormal findings for corrective action.”

Q55

What is thermography and why is it used?

+

Definition

Thermography is a non-contact condition-monitoring technique that uses infrared imaging to find abnormal temperature patterns.

Maintenance focus

Loose connections, overloaded feeders, contact resistance, busbar hotspots and phase imbalance. Scans should be done under reasonable load, as a lightly loaded joint can hide a defect.

Model interview answer

“If one MCC feeder termination is significantly hotter than similar feeders at comparable load, I suspect a loose termination, high contact resistance, overload or imbalance. I verify the load, isolate safely, correct the defect and perform a follow-up scan.”

Q56

How do you select a power cable?

+

Definition

Cable selection must satisfy current-carrying capacity (after derating), voltage drop, short-circuit withstand and installation conditions.

Formula

I_b ≤ I_n ≤ I_z I_2 ≤ 1.45 × I_z Adiabatic short-circuit check: S ≥ √(I² × t) / k

I_b = design current, I_n = protective device rating, I_z = derated cable capacity, I_2 = current ensuring effective device operation (IEC 60364 / BS 7671 approach). In the adiabatic check, S = conductor area (mm²), I = prospective fault current (A), t = device disconnection time (s, valid up to about 5 s) and k depends on conductor material, insulation and its initial/final temperatures.

Key points

  • Design load current
  • Copper or aluminium conductor
  • Installation method
  • Ambient temperature
  • Grouping
  • Derating factors
  • Voltage drop
  • Short-circuit withstand
  • Insulation type
  • Future load

Model interview answer

“I calculate design current, then select the cable considering derating, installation method, voltage drop and short-circuit withstand. I also verify that breaker and cable are properly coordinated.”

Q57

What causes cable overheating?

+

Causes

  • Overload
  • Undersized cable
  • Loose termination
  • Poor crimping
  • High ambient temperature
  • Cable grouping without derating
  • Harmonics (especially neutral current)
  • Damaged insulation

Model interview answer

“I compare actual current with the derated cable capacity and inspect the termination. If the cable body is normal but the lug is much hotter, I suspect contact resistance or poor crimping. Thermography is useful here.”

Q58

What is cable termination?

+

Definition

Cable termination is the process of safely connecting a cable to equipment using the correct lugs, glands, crimping and insulation.

Model interview answer

“I check correct lug size, conductor stripping, crimp tool and die, gland, heat-shrink or cold-shrink insulation, mechanical support and terminal torque. A poor termination creates high contact resistance and can eventually cause fire or equipment failure.”

Q59

What is electrical earthing?

+

Definition

Earthing provides a controlled path for fault current so protection operates quickly, and keeps exposed conductive parts at a safer potential.

Key points

  • Equipment (protective) earthing
  • System earthing (TN, TT, IT)
  • Earth continuity
  • Earth electrodes and grid
  • Equipotential bonding

Model interview answer

“I verify that exposed metallic parts are properly bonded to the earthing system. During maintenance I check earth continuity and measure earth resistance per the applicable site procedure and standard (e.g. fall-of-potential method).”

Q60

What are the basic electrical safety requirements during maintenance?

+

Key points

  • Permit to work (PTW)
  • Isolation from all sources
  • Lockout/tagout (LOTO)
  • Discharge stored energy (capacitors, cables)
  • Test for dead with a proven tester
  • Apply temporary earths where required
  • Appropriate PPE (including arc-flash rated)
  • Barricading
  • Authorised personnel only

Model interview answer

“My first priority is safety, not troubleshooting speed. I isolate the source, apply LOTO, discharge stored energy, and verify absence of voltage with a tester proven before and after use before starting work. I never bypass a safety interlock to restore production.”

Module 6 · Q61–Q70

Electrical Testing & Troubleshooting

Test instruments, commissioning tests and structured troubleshooting sequences.

Q61

What is a multimeter?

+

Definition

A multimeter measures electrical quantities such as AC/DC voltage, resistance and continuity, and many models also measure current, frequency and capacitance.

Model interview answer

“I select the correct function and range before testing. For live measurements I use an instrument with an appropriate CAT rating (e.g. CAT III/IV for distribution work) and proper PPE.”

Q62

What is a clamp meter?

+

Definition

A clamp meter measures current by sensing the magnetic field around a conductor, without breaking the circuit. True-RMS models are needed for non-sinusoidal currents such as VFD inputs.

Model interview answer

“It is ideal for measuring motor and feeder current in operation. I compare all three phase currents to identify imbalance without opening the circuit. Clamping only one conductor is essential — clamping around a whole cable reads roughly zero.”

Q63

What is a megger (insulation resistance tester)?

+

Definition

An insulation resistance tester applies a DC test voltage (commonly 500 V, 1 kV, 2.5 kV or 5 kV depending on equipment rating) and measures insulation resistance.

Applications

  • Motors
  • Cables
  • Transformers
  • Panels and switchgear

Model interview answer

“I isolate the equipment, disconnect sensitive electronics such as VFDs and PLC cards, and verify the circuit is safe before testing. I discharge the equipment after the test, record the IR value and compare it with history and the applicable equipment or manufacturer criteria.”

Q64

Explain continuity, polarity and phase-sequence testing.

+

Definition

Continuity confirms a complete electrical path, polarity confirms correct connection of line/neutral or DC +/−, and phase-sequence testing confirms the order of the three-phase supply.

Model interview answer

“Before commissioning I verify continuity and correct wiring, then confirm phase sequence. This is especially important for pumps, fans and compressors, where reverse rotation can cause damage.”

Q65

What electrical tests are performed before commissioning?

+

Key tests

  • Visual and mechanical inspection
  • Continuity
  • Insulation resistance
  • Earth continuity
  • Phase sequence
  • Protection testing (injection)
  • Control circuit testing
  • Functional/interlock testing
  • Motor rotation (uncoupled where possible)

Model interview answer

“I follow a structured pre-commissioning checklist and verify wiring, insulation, earthing, protection, control logic and motor rotation before allowing equipment to run under load.”

Q66

How do you troubleshoot a motor that suddenly trips?

+

Sequence

Trip indication → Supply → Isolation → Cable → Motor → Load → Protection settings → Test → Controlled restart

Model interview answer

“First I check the trip indication and whether it was overload, short circuit, earth fault or another protection. Then I check incoming voltage and phase balance, isolate the motor, test cable insulation and winding condition, and inspect bearings and mechanical load. I verify protection settings before a controlled restart.”

Q67

An MCC motor feeder is repeatedly tripping. How do you troubleshoot it?

+

Possible causes

  • Motor overload
  • Short circuit
  • Earth fault
  • Cable insulation failure
  • Mechanical jam
  • Bearing failure
  • Incorrect overload setting
  • Winding fault

Model interview answer

“I separate electrical and mechanical possibilities. I check the trip indication, measure operating current, inspect the driven equipment, test cable and motor insulation and verify overload settings. I do not keep resetting the feeder without identifying the cause.”

Q68

Why does a contactor chatter or burn?

+

Chatter causes

Low or unstable coil voltage, loose control wiring, wrong coil voltage, damaged coil or shading ring, auxiliary contact problems, mechanical obstruction or dirty pole faces.

Contact burning causes

Excessive current, frequent switching (inching/jogging), arcing, low contact pressure, or the wrong utilisation category (e.g. an AC-3 rating used for AC-4 duty).

Model interview answer

“If a contactor chatters, I first measure the coil voltage during operation, because unstable control voltage is a common cause. For burned contacts I check load current, switching frequency, contact condition and whether the contactor is correctly rated for the duty.”

Q69

How do you troubleshoot an electrical panel?

+

Sequence

Visual → Drawing → Incoming → Control supply → Protection → Components → Wiring → Load

Model interview answer

“I start with the symptom and the electrical drawing. Then I check incoming supply, control voltage, protection devices, contactors/relays, terminals and outgoing feeders in sequence. I use measurements to find the failed point rather than replacing components at random.”

Q70

Explain your complete breakdown troubleshooting method.

+

Sequence

Safety → Isolation → Understand the symptom → Visual inspection → Measurement → Root cause → Repair → Testing → Controlled restoration → Monitoring → Documentation

Model interview answer

“For repeated or critical failures I also perform a root cause analysis, so the same failure doesn't come back.”

Module 7 · Q71–Q80

Electronics & Instrumentation

Diodes, rectifiers, SMPS, relays, sensors, 4–20 mA loops and signal tracing.

Q71

What is a diode?

+

Definition

A diode is a semiconductor device that allows current to flow primarily in one direction.

Applications

  • Rectification
  • Reverse-polarity protection
  • Flyback (freewheeling) protection across relay coils
  • Switching

Model interview answer

“I use the multimeter's diode-test mode to check forward conduction (roughly 0.5–0.7 V for a silicon diode) and reverse blocking. A shorted or open diode can cause power-supply and control-circuit failures.”

Q72

What is a rectifier?

+

Definition

A rectifier converts AC into unidirectional (pulsating) DC, which is then smoothed by filtering.

Types

  • Half-wave
  • Full-wave (centre-tapped)
  • Bridge

Model interview answer

“Bridge rectifiers are common in industrial power supplies and drives, before DC filtering or switching stages. If a DC supply fails I check the input, rectifier, DC bus capacitor and downstream circuit.”

Q73

What is an SMPS?

+

Definition

A switched-mode power supply uses high-frequency switching to convert and regulate power efficiently.

Sequence

Input → Rectifier → Switching stage → Transformer/converter → Output → Feedback

Model interview answer

“When an SMPS fails I check the input fuse, rectifier, switching device, DC bus, output section and feedback circuit. I also check whether an external short or overload is making the supply shut down or hiccup.”

Q74

Relay vs SSR — what is the difference?

+

Definition

A conventional relay switches with electromechanical contacts; a solid-state relay switches with semiconductors.

Key points

  • Relay: physical contacts, galvanic isolation, contact wear, small leakage.
  • SSR: fast, silent, no contact wear, but has leakage current and voltage drop.
  • SSRs need heat dissipation (heatsink) and usually fail short-circuited.

Model interview answer

“I select the device by switching frequency, load type, voltage/current and environment. For high-frequency switching such as heater control an SSR may suit, but I check its thermal design.”

Q75

What is a proximity sensor?

+

Definition

A proximity sensor detects an object without physical contact.

Types

  • Inductive (metal targets)
  • Capacitive (most materials, including liquids)
  • Photoelectric
  • Ultrasonic

Model interview answer

“In plant automation, proximity sensors are used for position, counting and speed detection. If a sensor 'fails', I check supply voltage, sensing distance and target, LED indication, wiring and the PLC input before replacing it.”

Q76

Explain PNP and NPN sensors.

+

Definition

A PNP sensor sources positive voltage to the load/PLC input when active; an NPN sensor sinks current to 0 V when active.

Key points

  • PNP sensors pair with PLC sinking inputs (common to 0 V).
  • NPN sensors pair with PLC sourcing inputs (common to +24 V).
  • Naming conventions for 'sink' and 'source' inputs vary by manufacturer — follow the input wiring diagram.

Model interview answer

“Before replacing a sensor I verify whether the PLC input is configured for PNP or NPN. A mismatch means a perfectly healthy sensor never produces a PLC input signal.”

Q77

What is a 4–20 mA signal?

+

Definition

4–20 mA is a standard analog current signal for transmitting process variables in industry.

Formula

PV = LRV + (mA − 4) / 16 × (URV − LRV)

Example, 0–100 bar: 4 mA = 0 bar, 12 mA = 50 bar, 20 mA = 100 bar.

Model interview answer

“4–20 mA is robust against voltage drop and electrical noise and gives a 'live zero' — a reading near 0 mA indicates a broken loop rather than a zero process value. During troubleshooting I measure the actual loop current and compare it with the PLC/HMI value.”

Q78

What is an industrial transmitter?

+

Definition

A transmitter converts a physical process variable such as pressure, temperature, flow or level into a standardised signal (e.g. 4–20 mA, often with HART or a fieldbus).

Model interview answer

“For a transmitter fault I check the process condition, 24 V loop supply, wiring, loop current, calibration and PLC scaling. I don't immediately assume the transmitter itself is faulty.”

Q79

How do you troubleshoot a solenoid valve or sensor?

+

Model interview answer

“I follow the signal path from supply to field device to PLC I/O and then to the actuator. For a solenoid I check coil voltage, coil resistance, PLC output, interposing relay/DO module and mechanical valve operation (including air supply). For a sensor I check supply, output signal and PLC input.”

Q80

Explain instrumentation fault tracing.

+

Sequence

Sensor → Cable → Junction box → PLC/controller input → Logic → Output → Actuator

Model interview answer

“I trace the signal step by step instead of replacing instruments at random. If the HMI shows an incorrect pressure, I first verify the actual process and transmitter output, then measure the 4–20 mA signal at the JB and PLC input, and finally check PLC scaling.”

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Why should a CT secondary never be left open while the primary is carrying current? +

With primary current flowing, an open secondary removes the opposing ampere-turns, so the core saturates and a dangerously high voltage can appear across the secondary terminals. This creates a shock hazard and can damage the CT insulation and connected meters or relays. CT secondaries are shorted using an approved test block or shorting link before any meter or relay is disconnected.

Why are transformers rated in kVA and not kW? +

Transformer losses depend on voltage (core loss) and current (copper loss), not on the power factor of the connected load, and the manufacturer cannot know the load power factor in advance. Rating in kVA therefore describes the thermal capacity of the transformer regardless of whether the load is resistive or inductive.

What insulation resistance value is acceptable for a motor winding? +

There is no single universal number. IEEE 43 recommends minimum one-minute values corrected to 40 °C, for example about 100 MΩ for most form-wound AC windings built after about 1970 and about 5 MΩ for random-wound machines and form-wound coils rated below 1 kV. The manufacturer's criteria, the test voltage and the trend against earlier readings matter as much as the absolute value.

How do you convert a 4–20 mA signal into engineering units? +

Use PV = LRV + (mA − 4) ÷ 16 × (URV − LRV), where LRV and URV are the lower and upper range values of the transmitter. For a 0–100 bar transmitter, 12 mA corresponds to 50 bar. A reading close to 0 mA usually means a broken loop rather than a zero process value, which is why the live zero at 4 mA is useful.

What is the difference between an MCC and a PCC panel? +

An MCC (motor control centre) contains motor feeders with contactors, overload relays and control circuits for starting and protecting motors. A PCC (power control centre) distributes power to downstream boards and large loads through incomers, busbars and feeder breakers. Maintenance on an MCC focuses on starters and control wiring, while PCC maintenance focuses on breakers, busbars and load distribution.
