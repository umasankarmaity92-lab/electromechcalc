1. Introduction

What is a power transformer?

  • A static electrical device that transfers energy between two or more circuits by electromagnetic induction.
  • Steps voltage up or down; frequency stays the same.
  • Ideally input power = output power. Real losses: core, winding resistance, stray and dielectric losses.

Typical locations:

  • Generating stations (step-up)
  • Transmission and grid substations
  • Industrial substations
  • Large commercial facilities
  • Distribution networks supplying LV loads

Why transformer maintenance is different

  • No continuously rotating parts in the main energy-transfer system (unlike motors, pumps, compressors).
  • Auxiliary systems do have mechanical parts: OLTC, cooling fans, oil pumps, conservator/breather, valves, seals, linkages.
  • Main failure causes: insulation deterioration, oil contamination, thermal stress, electrical faults, moisture ingress, winding displacement.

Effective maintenance = 3 activities:

  1. Routine visual and mechanical inspection
  2. Insulating oil testing and condition monitoring
  3. Electrical diagnostic testing and protection-system verification

Why preventive maintenance pays off

  • Major transformers are high-value assets; replacement needs long procurement, transport and installation time.

Poor maintenance leads to:

  • Insulation breakdown
  • Internal winding faults
  • Oil degradation
  • Bushing failure
  • OLTC malfunction
  • Excessive operating temperature
  • Unplanned shutdown and production loss
  • Fire hazard and equipment damage

Scope: this guide focuses on oil-filled power and distribution transformers, with notes on dry-type units.

2. Equipment Overview & Types

Basic construction (oil-filled)

  1. Laminated magnetic core
  2. Primary and secondary windings
  3. Insulation system
  4. Main tank
  5. Insulating oil
  6. Conservator tank
  7. Silica gel breather
  8. Radiators / cooling banks
  9. Bushings
  10. Buchholz relay (where applicable)
  11. Tap changer
  12. Temperature indicators and protection devices
  • Core + windings = active part.
  • Insulation separates energised parts; oil gives insulation and cooling.

By construction

  • Core-type: windings around the core limbs; common in power and distribution transformers.
  • Shell-type: core surrounds a major part of the windings; used in specific power and special-purpose applications.

By cooling method

TypeMeaningHow heat is removed
ONANOil Natural Air NaturalNatural oil circulation; natural air cooling of radiators
ONAFOil Natural Air ForcedFans force air over radiators
OFAFOil Forced Air ForcedOil pumps + fans
OFWFOil Forced Water ForcedOil-to-water heat exchangers

More cooling stages = more fans, pumps, valves, controls and heat exchangers to maintain.

By application

  • Power transformers: generation, transmission, major industrial systems.
  • Distribution transformers: supply consumer/industrial loads. Common ratings in India include 11 kV/433 V, subject to utility and equipment specifications.
  • Auto-transformers: common winding for primary and secondary.
  • Furnace transformers: high-current furnace duty.
  • Rectifier transformers: supply industrial rectifier systems.
  • Special-purpose transformers: designed for specific duties.

Power vs distribution classification depends on application and design — not one universal voltage threshold. State the nominal LV system voltage and the transformer nameplate rating separately where relevant.

Oil-filled vs dry-type

Oil-filled:

  • Oil for cooling and insulation
  • Needs oil quality and moisture monitoring
  • Inspect conservator, breather, gaskets, radiators
  • Common in outdoor substations and industrial plants

Dry-type (cast-resin / VPI):

  • Solid insulation, no oil
  • Regular cleaning and inspection of insulation surfaces
  • Check ventilation and cooling
  • Partial discharge monitoring or other condition tests, depending on design

Transformer nameplate data

  • Rated capacity (kVA/MVA)
  • HV and LV rated voltage
  • Rated current
  • Frequency
  • Vector group
  • Percentage impedance
  • Cooling class
  • Temperature rise
  • Insulation level
  • Tap changer range
  • Oil quantity
  • Manufacturer and serial number

Read the nameplate before any test: vector group, impedance, cooling class and tap range decide test connections and how results are interpreted.

3. Working Principle

Electromagnetic induction

  • AC in the primary produces changing flux in the laminated core.
  • Flux links the secondary and induces an EMF.
  • Induced voltage depends on number of turns.
V₁ / V₂ = N₁ / N₂
  • V₁, V₂ = primary / secondary voltage; N₁, N₂ = primary / secondary turns.

Current relationship (ideal transformer)

I₁ / I₂ = N₂ / N₁
  • Voltage up → current down, for roughly the same power transferred.

Why the core is laminated

  • Thin, insulated electrical-grade silicon-steel sheets restrict eddy-current paths and reduce eddy-current loss.
  • The insulation coating between laminations prevents electrical contact between adjacent sheets and helps limit circulating eddy currents.

Maintenance points:

  • Core insulation condition
  • Core-to-earth insulation
  • Proper core grounding
  • Signs of abnormal core heating
  • Any unintended additional core earth connection

Where the design provides an accessible core earth connection, maintain the intended single-point grounding. Investigate any unintended additional grounding point — it may create circulating currents and localised heating.

Losses and cooling

LossCause
Core (iron) lossHysteresis + eddy currents
Copper (winding) lossConductor resistance; increases with load current
Stray load lossStray flux in structural parts
Dielectric lossLosses in the insulation
  • All losses become heat, which the cooling system must remove.
  • Excess temperature speeds up thermal ageing of cellulose insulation.

Rule of thumb: insulation life roughly halves for each ~6–8 °C rise. This is only indicative; actual ageing depends on insulation system, temperature, moisture and oxygen.

On-load tap changing (OLTC)

Functions:

  • Keeps secondary voltage in range
  • Compensates for supply-voltage variation
  • Handles voltage changes due to load
  • Supports automatic voltage regulation (AVR)
  • Changes the winding turns ratio while energised and on load.
  • Moving contacts and switching parts wear electrically and mechanically.
  • Plan OLTC maintenance by manufacturer (OEM) instructions, operation count and condition monitoring.

4. Major Components & Their Functions

4.1 Core

Function: magnetic path for the alternating flux.

Check:

  • Abnormal noise and vibration
  • Core earth connection and single-point grounding (where the design provides access)
  • Abnormal core temperature
  • Core-to-earth insulation resistance, where an accessible test connection is provided, measured only after following the manufacturer’s isolation and core-grounding procedure

4.2 Windings

Function: transfer energy by induction; copper or aluminium conductors with paper/pressboard insulation.

Check:

  • Winding temperature
  • Insulation resistance
  • Winding resistance
  • Turns ratio (when required)
  • DGA for developing winding faults
  • Abnormal phase-current imbalance

4.3 Main tank

Function: houses core, windings and oil.

Check:

  • Oil leakage
  • Weld joints and gasketed connections
  • Corrosion
  • Tank earthing
  • Pressure-relief device in service
  • Tank deformation

4.4 Conservator tank

Function: expansion vessel above the tank; absorbs oil volume change with temperature, keeps tank oil level, limits oil contact with moist air (air cell/diaphragm designs do this best, but not every transformer has one).

Check:

  • Oil level
  • Tank leakage
  • Air-cell / diaphragm condition (where fitted)
  • Connecting pipes and isolation valves
  • Oil-level indicator operation

4.5 Silica gel breather

Function: lets air in/out of the conservator; silica gel absorbs moisture from incoming air.

Check:

  • Silica gel colour
  • Replace or regenerate saturated gel per manufacturer instructions
  • Breather oil cup (where fitted)
  • Air passage not blocked
  • Breather pipe leakage or damage

Silica gel colour change depends on the indicator type. Always follow the manufacturer’s colour indication.

4.6 Buchholz relay

Function: gas-actuated protection in the pipe between tank and conservator, fitted only on applicable conservator-type oil-filled transformers. Alarm = gas collecting from a developing fault; Trip = sudden oil surge from a severe internal fault.

Check:

  • Housing leakage
  • Alarm and trip contacts
  • Wiring
  • Mechanical condition
  • Gas accumulation indication
  • Test alarm/trip circuits per approved protection-testing procedure

Never reset or ignore a Buchholz alarm without finding the cause.

4.7 On-load tap changer (OLTC)

Function: changes the voltage ratio without interrupting load.

Check:

  • Operation counter
  • Drive motor and gear mechanism
  • Mechanical linkages
  • Diverter switch compartment oil
  • OLTC oil condition
  • Contact wear per OEM
  • Electrical and mechanical interlocks
  • AVR operation

Oil compartment arrangement varies by design — not every OLTC has a separate one. Follow the OEM manual.

4.8 Radiators & cooling system

Function: transfer heat from oil to surrounding air.

Check:

  • Dirt and blockage on fins
  • Leaks at radiator joints
  • Radiator isolation valves in correct position
  • Trapped air (where applicable)
  • Cooling fans
  • Pump operation (forced-oil systems)
  • Automatic cooling-stage controls

4.9 Bushings

Function: insulated connection between internal windings and external circuits (porcelain, RIP, OIP types).

Check:

  • Clean external insulation
  • Cracks and damage
  • Oil leakage
  • Terminal connections
  • Oil level (where applicable)
  • Capacitance and tan delta on suitable bushings

4.10 Temperature indicators

  • OTI (Oil Temperature Indicator): top-oil temperature.
  • WTI (Winding Temperature Indicator): indicates winding temperature, generally by a thermal-image (thermal replica) simulation: top-oil temperature plus a heating element fed by load current. Implementation varies with OEM design.

Check:

  • Temperature readings
  • Alarm and trip contacts
  • Auxiliary supply (where applicable)
  • Compare with independent measurement when required
  • Sensor and capillary condition

4.11 Pressure relief device (PRD)

Function: protects the tank from excessive internal pressure.

Check:

  • Housing
  • Alarm/trip indication and electrical contact circuit (not every PRD has electrical contacts)
  • Discharge path unobstructed
  • Device condition after any operation

4.12 Marshalling box & control cabinet

Function: houses control, indication and auxiliary wiring.

Check:

  • Moisture ingress
  • Space heaters (where fitted)
  • Cable glands and seals
  • Terminal tightness (approved procedure)
  • Loose wiring, damaged insulation
  • Insect and rodent ingress
  • Control fuses, MCBs, auxiliary supplies

5. Preventive Maintenance

Goal: find and fix defects before they become serious failures.

5.1 Transformer oil

  • Periodic oil quality tests
  • Maintain oil level
  • Track BDV and moisture trends
  • Monitor acidity and other ageing indicators
  • Check for contamination
  • Maintain the breather system
  • Investigate recurring leaks
  • Filter or dehydrate only when test results justify it

Choose filtration to match the problem. Filtration does not reverse chemical ageing or remove all dissolved gases.

  • Oil filtration, vacuum dehydration, reclamation and degassing are different processes.
  • Select the treatment from oil test results and the transformer OEM recommendation.

5.2 Gaskets and seals

Inspect:

  • Main tank cover
  • Conservator connections
  • Radiator flanges
  • Bushing turrets
  • Valve connections
  • Inspection covers
  • OLTC compartment

Do:

  • Check for oil seepage
  • Check gasket hardening or deterioration
  • Replace damaged gaskets
  • Use OEM-approved gasket material
  • Tighten bolts to specified torque

Avoid indiscriminate re-tightening — excess torque can damage the gasket or distort the flange.

5.3 Cooling system

  • Clean radiator fins
  • Check fan motor operation, bearings and blades
  • Inspect pump seals and bearings
  • Check oil circulation
  • Verify automatic cooling-stage operation
  • Test manual and automatic control modes

5.4 Bushings

  • Clean porcelain/composite surfaces
  • Check cracks and surface damage
  • Inspect terminal connections
  • Check oil level (applicable designs)
  • Check leakage
  • Electrical diagnostic tests by bushing type and voltage class

5.5 OLTC

Base OLTC maintenance on the OEM operation count, time interval and condition monitoring.

  • Record operation count
  • Check motor drive and linkages
  • Check position indication
  • Monitor diverter compartment oil
  • Inspect switching contacts during scheduled service
  • Check control and interlock circuits
  • Test AVR

5.6 Documentation

Keep a record per transformer:

  • Nameplate data
  • Inspection reports
  • Oil test and DGA reports
  • Electrical test results
  • OLTC operation count
  • Protection relay test records
  • Fault and trip history
  • Filtration and oil replacement records
  • Repair and shutdown history

Trend analysis is more useful than reading a single test result alone.

5.7 Loading and overload monitoring

  • Record phase-wise load current against rated current
  • Watch OTI/WTI trends at load
  • Sustained overload raises temperature and speeds insulation ageing
  • Review loading against nameplate rating and cooling stage in service
  • Use IEC 60076-7:2018 (loading guide for oil-immersed power transformers) together with the transformer manufacturer’s loading limits and applicable site requirements for planned overloads

5.8 Online condition monitoring

  • Options for critical units: online DGA, moisture-in-oil and temperature monitoring
  • Set alarm levels and trend the data
  • It supplements scheduled inspection and testing; it does not replace them

5.9 Commissioning records as baseline

  • Keep factory and commissioning reports (SFRA, winding resistance, turns ratio, tan delta, oil results)
  • Later test results are judged against these baselines

6. Predictive Maintenance & Oil Testing

Classification: oil quality tests (BDV, moisture, acidity, IFT, resistivity, dissipation factor) assess the condition of the insulating liquid. DGA and furan analysis are diagnostic tests that give information about possible internal faults and cellulose insulation ageing. Interpret them together with operating history and other condition-monitoring results.

Set oil-test intervals using IEC 60422 (oil quality), IEC 60599 / IEEE C57.104 (DGA), utility requirements, criticality and OEM recommendations.

6.1 Transformer oil sampling procedure

BDV and DGA results are only as good as the sample.

Collect insulating oil samples in accordance with IEC 60475 and the transformer manufacturer’s approved sampling procedure. Use appropriate sampling equipment and containers for the specific test, particularly DGA.

  1. Identify the correct sampling valve
  2. Check the transformer operating condition
  3. Clean the sampling valve and surrounding area
  4. Flush the sampling point per the approved sampling procedure
  5. Use clean, dry, suitable sampling equipment
  6. Avoid air bubbles and moisture contamination
  7. Use a gas-tight syringe or suitable container for DGA samples
  8. Label: transformer ID, date, time, oil temperature, sampling location
  9. Send to an appropriate testing laboratory
  10. Record results for trend analysis

6.2 Breakdown Voltage (BDV) test

  • Measures oil’s ability to withstand electrical stress before breakdown.
  • Mainly affected by moisture and suspended contaminants.

Procedure:

  1. Collect sample in a clean, dry container (representative, no contamination).
  2. Fill the approved test vessel.
  3. Maintain the specified electrode gap.
  4. Perform the test in accordance with IEC 60156:2025, using the specified test apparatus, electrode arrangement, sample preparation and test procedure.
  5. Record breakdown voltages.
  6. Repeat as required by the standard.
  7. Calculate and record the average.

Falling BDV may mean:

  • Moisture ingress
  • Particle contamination
  • Poor oil handling
  • Reduced oil cleanliness

Action:

  • Find the moisture entry point
  • Check breather and gaskets
  • Filter or dehydrate as suitable
  • Re-test after treatment

6.3 Dissolved Gas Analysis (DGA)

  • One of the most important methods for detecting developing internal faults.
  • Gas chromatography measures dissolved gases in oil.
GasPossible indication
Hydrogen (H₂)Partial discharge or electrical/thermal activity
Methane (CH₄)Low-temperature thermal fault or partial discharge
Ethane (C₂H₆)Lower-temperature thermal fault
Ethylene (C₂H₄)Higher-temperature thermal fault
Acetylene (C₂H₂)High-energy discharge or arcing
Carbon monoxide (CO)Cellulose insulation degradation
Carbon dioxide (CO₂)Paper ageing and oxidation

Reading the gases:

  • Hydrogen relates to several types of electrical fault
  • Methane and ethane can indicate thermal decomposition
  • Ethylene is a key indicator of high-temperature thermal faults; acetylene of electrical arcing
  • CO and CO₂ help assess cellulose insulation degradation

Interpretation methods:

  • IEEE C57.104
  • IEC 60599
  • Rogers Ratio
  • Duval Triangle
  • Key Gas Method

Consider together:

  • Absolute gas concentrations
  • Rate of gas generation
  • Gas ratios
  • Previous results
  • Loading and operating history
  • Sampling quality

Never confirm an internal fault from a single gas in isolation.

6.4 Moisture content

  • Measured in ppm, commonly by Karl Fischer titration.
  • Affects both oil dielectric strength and paper insulation life.

Sources:

  • Atmospheric ingress
  • Deteriorated gaskets
  • Saturated silica gel
  • Poor oil handling
  • Cellulose ageing

Action:

  • Inspect breather
  • Check for leaks
  • Find ingress point
  • Dehydrate oil where appropriate
  • Assess paper moisture with suitable diagnostic methods

6.5 Acidity (neutralisation value)

  • Expressed in mg KOH/g; rising value = oil oxidation and ageing.
  • Consequences: sludge, reduced cooling efficiency, poorer oil quality, faster insulation ageing.

Action:

  • Monitor trend
  • Inspect cooling system
  • Check oxygen and moisture ingress
  • Evaluate reclamation or replacement if justified

6.6 Interfacial Tension (IFT)

  • Measures tension between oil and water.
  • Falling IFT = polar contaminants and ageing products.
  • Read together with acidity and other oil tests.

6.7 Furan analysis

  • Assesses cellulose insulation degradation; 2-FAL is the key compound.
  • Consider design, oil processing history, oil replacement/reclamation, previous results and other diagnostics.
  • It is a condition-assessment tool, not a direct measure of remaining life.

6.8 Insulation Resistance (IR) and Polarization Index (PI)

  • IR: winding-to-winding and winding-to-earth, using an approved insulation tester.
  • Record readings at 1 minute and 10 minutes.
PI = IR(10 min) / IR(1 min)

Precautions:

  • Record winding and oil temperature
  • Isolate external connections as required
  • Use the correct test voltage
  • Discharge windings after the test
  • Compare with historical values

PI ≥ 2 is not a universal pass criterion for every oil-filled transformer. Interpret by design, insulation system and applicable test guidance.

7. Transformer Electrical Testing

Done at commissioning, scheduled condition assessment, after major maintenance or after a suspected fault.

  • IR/PI results cannot be judged by one acceptance criterion for all transformers.
  • SFRA results must be compared with a factory baseline or a suitable previous reference.
  • Earth resistance acceptance depends on site earthing design and applicable regulations.

7.1 Tan delta / dissipation factor

Detects:

  • Insulation deterioration
  • Moisture contamination
  • Insulation ageing
  • Abnormal dielectric losses

Do:

  • Isolate the transformer
  • Follow approved test configuration and voltage
  • Record capacitance and dissipation factor
  • Compare with factory and previous reports

7.2 Sweep Frequency Response Analysis (SFRA)

Detects (mechanical integrity):

  • Winding displacement
  • Winding deformation
  • Core movement
  • Transport damage
  • Changes after high-current through-faults
  • Compare the response with the factory baseline or a suitable previous reference.

7.3 Excitation current

Purpose:

  • Find core abnormalities
  • Find winding-related abnormalities
  • Investigate phase-current asymmetry
  • Assess magnetic circuit condition
  • Interpret by design, test connection and tap position.
  • An excitation-current result alone cannot confirm a fault; read it with other tests (turns ratio, winding resistance, SFRA, DGA).

7.4 Winding resistance

Detects:

  • Loose connections
  • High-resistance joints
  • Broken conductor strands
  • OLTC contact-related abnormalities

Precautions:

  • Record winding temperature
  • Correct values to a common reference temperature
  • Let readings stabilise
  • Follow instrument manufacturer’s procedure
  • Demagnetise after testing when required

7.5 Transformer Turns Ratio (TTR)

Purpose:

  • Identify ratio deviations, incorrect winding connections and possible winding abnormalities, including significant shorted-turn conditions where detectable
  • Identify wrong winding connections
  • Verify tap positions
  • Confirm ratio against nameplate
  • Compare per phase and per tap, considering tolerance and vector group.

7.6 OLTC contact resistance

Purpose:

  • Contact deterioration
  • Abnormal contact resistance
  • Switching-contact condition
  • Overheating risk
  • Use a micro-ohmmeter and an OEM-approved procedure; method must suit the OLTC design.

7.7 Bushing capacitance and tan delta

  • Measure capacitance, power factor, dissipation factor.
  • Significant deviation from factory or previous data → investigate further.

7.8 Surge arrester inspection

  • Protects insulation against lightning and switching overvoltages.
  • Inspect housing; check cracks and contamination
  • Inspect earth connections
  • Record surge counter readings
  • Check leakage current where suitable
  • Investigate abnormal indications

7.9 Earthing resistance and neutral grounding

  • Inspect tank earthing and neutral grounding connections
  • Check earth conductors for corrosion
  • Measure earth resistance by an appropriate method
  • Inspect neutral grounding resistors (where fitted)
  • Compare with site design requirements

Acceptable value depends on earthing design and regulations — do not assume one universal resistance value.

7.10 Protection relay testing

Functions to test:

  • Differential (87T)
  • Restricted Earth Fault (REF)
  • Overcurrent (50/51)
  • Earth fault (50N/51N)
  • Buchholz
  • Pressure relief
  • Oil temperature alarm/trip
  • Winding temperature alarm/trip

Do:

  • Check relay settings
  • Verify CT ratios and polarity
  • Secondary injection test
  • Verify trip circuit operation
  • Check alarm annunciation
  • Verify breaker tripping and interlocking
  • Record results
  • Follow the approved protection coordination study and relay manufacturer’s procedure.

7.11 Cooling system functional test

  • Fan start/stop
  • Pump start/stop
  • Automatic cooling-stage selection
  • Temperature-based control
  • Manual operation
  • Motor protection
  • Control circuit interlocks
  • Alarm and indication circuits

Set actual frequency by transformer criticality, operating environment, loading, historical test results, OEM recommendations and applicable regulations. IR/PI, DGA, winding resistance and OLTC servicing are not mandatory annual activities for every transformer.

Mainly a visual and operational check. Record important readings in the daily logbook.

9. Weekly Inspection

Focus: external components and auxiliary systems.

  • Bushing surfaces: contamination and cracks
  • Radiator fins: dust and blockage
  • Visible tank and radiator earth connections
  • Marshalling box and control cabinet
  • Moisture ingress
  • Cable glands and enclosure seals
  • Cooling fan motor noise
  • Fan blades and guards
  • Abnormal pump noise
  • Alarm annunciation review
  • Accessible electrical connections: signs of overheating
  • Surroundings: fire hazards and obstructions

10. Monthly Inspection

  • Silica gel: replace or regenerate when required
  • Record OLTC operation counter
  • Review OLTC maintenance due status
  • External OLTC drive mechanism
  • Motor drive and position indication
  • Oil leakage history
  • Protection relay indications
  • Auxiliary supply and control circuits
  • Radiator valves and cooling pipes
  • Temperature trends
  • Accessible cable terminations
  • Corrosion on tank, radiators, conservator
  • Loading vs rated capacity

Thermography: do it under suitable operating load and per the site thermal inspection plan. A monthly spot-check can supplement a periodic full survey.

11. Quarterly & Half-Yearly Inspection

11.1 Quarterly (critical / high-priority transformers)

  1. Infrared thermography of accessible electrical connections
  2. Bushing terminals and cable connections
  3. Radiator temperature distribution
  4. Cooling fan and pump operation
  5. Automatic cooling-stage controls
  6. Oil leakage and abnormal temperature trends
  7. Protection alarm history
  8. Oil quality spot-checks where the condition-monitoring plan requires

11.2 Half-yearly (by criticality and previous results)

  1. Selected oil quality tests
  2. Moisture and BDV trends
  3. Acidity and other ageing indicators (where applicable)
  4. Insulation resistance test when justified
  5. Detailed bushing inspection
  6. Earth connections and earthing system
  7. Cooling-system controls
  8. OLTC operating mechanism
  9. Review previous six months of records
  10. Investigate any abnormal DGA trend

Half-yearly testing is not a universal mandatory interval. Decide using IEC 60422, applicable standards, transformer condition, environment and OEM recommendations.

12. Annual Maintenance

A comprehensive check of transformer condition, protection systems and auxiliary equipment.

The inspection and testing intervals in this guide are indicative. Set actual frequency by transformer criticality, operating environment, loading, historical test results, OEM recommendations and applicable regulations. IR/PI, DGA, winding resistance and OLTC servicing are not mandatory annual activities for every transformer.

12.1 Pre-maintenance

  1. Review last year’s maintenance history
  2. Check loading history
  3. Review DGA and oil test reports
  4. List outstanding defects
  5. Prepare shutdown plan
  6. Obtain permits
  7. Arrange test equipment and spares
  8. Confirm isolation and earthing arrangements

12.2 Physical inspection

  • Main tank
  • Conservator and breather
  • Radiators and valves
  • Bushings
  • Gaskets and oil leakage
  • Cooling fans and pumps
  • Marshalling box
  • Cable connections
  • Earthing system
  • Pressure relief and protection devices

12.3 Electrical testing

Select tests by transformer design, condition and maintenance scope.

  • Insulation resistance and PI
  • Winding resistance
  • Turns ratio
  • Excitation current
  • Tan delta and capacitance
  • SFRA (where required)
  • Bushing diagnostic testing
  • OLTC diagnostic testing
  • Protection relay testing
  • Trip circuit verification

12.4 Oil testing

  • BDV
  • DGA
  • Moisture content
  • Acidity
  • Interfacial tension
  • Dissipation factor
  • Resistivity
  • Furan analysis (where appropriate)

Choose the test suite by transformer condition and previous results.

12.5 Protection and functional testing

  1. Differential protection
  2. REF protection
  3. Overcurrent and earth fault protection
  4. Buchholz alarm and trip circuits
  5. PRD alarm/trip contacts
  6. OTI and WTI contacts
  7. Cooling fan and pump control circuits
  8. OLTC control and interlocks
  9. Breaker trip circuits
  10. Record all results

12.6 Post-maintenance

  1. Remove all temporary test connections
  2. Remove isolation and earthing per approved procedure
  3. Check oil levels
  4. Verify valve positions
  5. Confirm protection circuits restored
  6. Confirm cooling systems available
  7. Complete pre-energisation checklist
  8. Energise per approved switching procedure
  9. Monitor voltage, current, temperature, noise
  10. Record post-energisation observations

12.7 OLTC major maintenance

Do not open or service an OLTC based only on a generic annual interval. Follow OEM operation count, calendar interval or diagnostic condition.

  • Diverter switch and contact inspection; replace contacts if necessary
  • Oil compartment inspection; oil replacement or treatment as specified
  • Drive mechanism, gear and linkage inspection
  • Functional testing
  • Position indication verification

13. Common Faults & Troubleshooting

13.1 High oil or winding temperature

Possible causes:

  • Overload
  • Cooling fan failure
  • Oil pump failure
  • Blocked radiator fins
  • Low oil level
  • Wrong radiator valve position
  • Internal electrical fault
  • High ambient temperature

Troubleshooting steps:

  1. Check load and phase currents
  2. Compare OTI and WTI
  3. Check fans
  4. Check oil pumps (where fitted)
  5. Inspect radiator surfaces
  6. Verify oil level
  7. Review DGA if internal fault suspected

Corrective action: Restore cooling, investigate overload, arrange diagnostic testing if temperature stays abnormal.

13.2 Buchholz relay alarm

Possible causes:

  • Gas from an internal fault
  • Insulation deterioration
  • Local overheating
  • Partial discharge
  • Air trapped after oil filling or maintenance

Troubleshooting steps:

  1. Record relay indication
  2. Check transformer operating condition
  3. Inspect for oil leakage
  4. Collect and analyse gas by approved procedure
  5. Conduct DGA
  6. Find the gas source

Corrective action: Follow the site protection response procedure. Do not reset or re-energise without investigation and authorisation by a qualified protection/transformer engineer.

A Buchholz trip is a serious internal-fault indication.

13.3 Low oil BDV

Possible causes:

  • Moisture
  • Dust and particles
  • Poor oil handling
  • Deteriorated seals
  • Breather malfunction

Troubleshooting steps:

  1. Collect a representative sample
  2. Repeat BDV by standard method
  3. Check moisture content
  4. Inspect silica gel
  5. Check gaskets and seals
  6. Review previous oil reports

Corrective action: Filter or dehydrate oil as appropriate and trace the contamination source.

13.4 Increasing oil acidity

Possible causes:

  • Oil oxidation
  • High operating temperature
  • Moisture and oxygen ingress
  • Oil ageing

Troubleshooting steps:

  1. Check acidity trend
  2. Review operating temperature
  3. Inspect conservator and breather
  4. Check oil sealing
  5. Review IFT and other oil results

Corrective action: Evaluate oil reclamation or replacement per applicable standard and oil condition.

13.5 Abnormal DGA results

Possible causes:

  • Partial discharge
  • Thermal overheating
  • Arcing
  • Winding insulation deterioration
  • Core-related faults

Troubleshooting steps:

  1. Verify sample quality
  2. Compare with previous reports
  3. Calculate gas generation rates
  4. Interpret gas ratios
  5. Apply suitable DGA methods
  6. Run extra electrical tests if needed

Corrective action: Increase monitoring frequency and escalate for engineering assessment.

Suspected active arcing or rapidly rising fault gases need urgent attention.

13.6 Bushing overheating or tracking

Possible causes:

  • Loose terminals
  • Surface contamination
  • Cracked insulation
  • Internal insulation deterioration
  • Excessive electrical stress

Troubleshooting steps:

  1. Thermography check
  2. Inspect external surfaces
  3. Check terminals under approved isolation
  4. Review capacitance and tan delta results
  5. Investigate leakage

Corrective action: Clean surfaces, fix connections under safe isolation, replace defective bushings when required.

13.7 OLTC malfunction

Possible causes:

  • Drive motor failure
  • Linkage problems
  • Diverter contact deterioration
  • Control circuit failure
  • Wrong interlocking
  • Mechanical jamming

Troubleshooting steps:

  1. Check tap position indication
  2. Check motor supply
  3. Inspect control fuses and contactors
  4. Review operation counter
  5. Check linkage
  6. Review OLTC diagnostic and oil results

Corrective action: Isolate as required and arrange OEM-approved OLTC inspection.

Never force a jammed mechanism.

13.8 Oil leakage

Possible causes:

  • Damaged gaskets
  • Loose or distorted flanges
  • Corrosion
  • Damaged valves
  • Bushing seal deterioration

Troubleshooting steps:

  1. Locate the leak
  2. Assess rate of oil loss
  3. Check oil level
  4. Inspect gasket and flange
  5. Check surrounding structure for corrosion

Corrective action: Repair or replace the seal or component with approved materials and procedures.

13.9 Abnormal noise

Possible causes:

  • Normal magnetostriction humming
  • Loose external fittings
  • Fan or pump vibration
  • Core clamping problems
  • Internal electrical discharge

Troubleshooting steps:

  1. Compare with normal operating noise
  2. Inspect external fittings
  3. Check fan and pump vibration
  4. Review load current
  5. Investigate crackling or discharge-like sounds immediately
  6. DGA and extra diagnostics if internal fault suspected

Corrective action: Identify the source before any repair.

Crackling, smoke or other signs of active discharge need an immediate safety and protection response.

13.10 Differential protection trip

Possible causes:

  • Internal winding fault
  • Phase-to-phase fault
  • Earth fault inside protected zone
  • CT circuit problems
  • Wrong CT polarity or wiring
  • Wrong relay settings
  • Inrush-related operation or restraint problem

Troubleshooting steps:

  1. Record relay event and fault data
  2. Check protection indications
  3. Inspect external CT circuits where safe
  4. Review differential and restraint values
  5. Conduct transformer electrical tests
  6. Perform DGA where appropriate

Corrective action: Do not re-energise until the cause is found and a qualified protection/transformer engineer has investigated and authorised restoration.

14. Safety Precautions

Transformer work involves lethal voltages, stored energy, combustible oil and heavy equipment. Only trained and authorised personnel may do it.

14.1 Lockout/Tagout (LOTO)

  1. Identify all power sources
  2. Isolate HV and LV connections
  3. Isolate auxiliary and control supplies where required
  4. Prevent back-feeding
  5. Apply lockout and tagout
  6. Verify absence of voltage with an approved detector
  7. Discharge and earth per approved procedure
  8. Establish permit-to-work boundary

14.2 Induced voltage and back-feed protection

  • Isolate all possible sources of supply
  • Consider induced voltage from nearby energised circuits
  • Verify absence of voltage at all relevant terminals
  • Apply approved temporary earthing
  • Prevent back-feeding through LV generators, solar systems or interconnected networks

14.3 PPE (per task risk assessment)

  • Arc-rated clothing
  • Voltage-rated insulating gloves, with leather protectors where required, selected by voltage level and task risk assessment
  • Safety helmet and shoes
  • Eye and face protection
  • Hearing protection
  • Chemical-resistant gloves for oil handling

14.4 Oil handling

  • Use approved sampling equipment; avoid contamination
  • Prevent spills; use suitable storage containers
  • Follow approved disposal procedures
  • Verify PCB status on legacy equipment
  • Maintain suitable fire protection
  • Sampling errors spoil test results — follow section 6.1

14.5 Electrical testing safety

  • Isolate the transformer
  • Disconnect external equipment where required
  • Follow instrument manufacturer’s instructions
  • Maintain safe test boundaries
  • Discharge tested windings
  • Apply temporary earths as specified
  • Restore all connections before energising

14.6 Confined space / tank entry

  • Confined-space entry permit
  • Atmosphere testing and oxygen monitoring
  • Gas hazard assessment
  • Suitable ventilation
  • Standby personnel and rescue arrangements
  • Approved electrical isolation and earthing

Never enter a transformer tank without an approved confined-space procedure.

14.7 Fire safety

  • Fire detection systems
  • Suitable fire suppression
  • Oil containment
  • Emergency-response access
  • Clear area around the transformer
  • Follow local fire regulations and site rules

14.8 OLTC mechanical safety

  • Isolate OLTC drive supply and apply LOTO
  • Verify mechanical isolation
  • Prevent unexpected motor operation
  • Follow OEM instructions for manual operation

16. Mechanical Aspects of Transformer Care

Mechanical parts strongly affect reliability and life, even though the transformer is mainly an electrical asset.

16.1 Cooling fans (ONAF and other forced-air systems)

  • Fan blades
  • Motor bearing condition
  • Motor current
  • Electrical terminals
  • Vibration and abnormal noise
  • Automatic starting
  • Fan guards and mounting bolts

16.2 Oil pumps (forced-oil systems)

  • Operating condition
  • Vibration and noise
  • Mechanical seals
  • Motor current
  • Oil circulation
  • Leakage
  • Automatic start/stop controls

16.3 OLTC mechanical parts

  • Drive motor, gearbox, operating shaft
  • Mechanical linkage, position indicator, limit switches
  • Diverter mechanism
  • Service only at the manufacturer’s interval and procedure

16.4 Radiators and structure

  • Radiator mounting bolts and supports
  • Flange connections
  • Corrosion
  • Oil leakage
  • Expansion joints
  • Tank mounting arrangements

16.5 Conservator and breather assembly

  • Conservator support
  • Oil-level indicator
  • Air-cell or diaphragm
  • Breather pipe, silica gel, oil cup
  • Connecting flanges and valves

Mechanical defects here can lead to moisture ingress and insulation deterioration.

17. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the acceptable BDV value for transformer oil?+

The acceptable BDV depends on transformer voltage class, oil type, electrode gap, test method and oil condition. IEC 60156 specifies the test method, while IEC 60422 provides guidance for evaluating in-service mineral insulating oil. Always compare results with the applicable equipment category and standard rather than using one universal BDV limit.

How frequently should transformer oil be tested?+

The testing frequency depends on transformer voltage class, age, loading, operating environment, criticality and previous oil-test results. Follow the applicable oil-maintenance guidance, utility requirements and transformer manufacturer’s recommendations. Critical or deteriorating transformers may require more frequent testing.

What causes transformer oil BDV to decrease?+

Transformer oil BDV can decrease due to moisture, suspended particles, dust, contamination, poor oil handling and deterioration of the insulation system. A faulty breather, damaged gasket or leaking seal may also allow moisture to enter the transformer.

What is the purpose of DGA testing?+

Dissolved Gas Analysis (DGA) detects and helps assess developing internal faults in oil-filled transformers. Different gases may indicate partial discharge, overheating, arcing or cellulose insulation degradation. Results should be interpreted using gas concentrations, generation rates, gas ratios and historical trends.

What is the difference between OTI and WTI?+

OTI measures the transformer’s top-oil temperature. WTI estimates winding temperature, generally using a thermal replica system that combines top-oil temperature with a heating element supplied by a load-current signal. Both may operate alarm and trip contacts, depending on the design.

What is the function of a Buchholz relay?+

A Buchholz relay is a gas-actuated protection device installed in the pipe between the main tank and conservator of applicable oil-filled transformers. Gas accumulation from a developing internal fault can operate its alarm contact, while a sudden oil surge may operate its trip contact.

Why is silica gel used in a transformer breather?+

Silica gel absorbs moisture from the air entering the conservator when transformer oil volume decreases. This helps reduce moisture contamination of the insulating oil and paper insulation. Regenerate or replace the silica gel according to its condition and the manufacturer’s instructions.

What is the purpose of an OLTC?+

An On-Load Tap Changer (OLTC) changes the effective winding turns ratio while the transformer remains energised and supplies its load. It helps regulate secondary voltage as the supply voltage or load conditions change.

What causes transformer overheating?+

Common causes include overloading, high ambient temperature, blocked radiators, cooling fan or pump failure, low oil level, incorrect radiator valve position and internal electrical faults. Check loading, temperature indications, cooling operation and oil condition before deciding corrective action.

Why is transformer earthing important?+

Transformer tank earthing provides a low-impedance path for fault current and helps protect personnel and equipment. Core and neutral grounding arrangements are design-specific and must follow the manufacturer’s instructions and the site’s earthing design.

What is the purpose of the PI test?+

The Polarization Index test assesses insulation behaviour by comparing insulation resistance readings at different time intervals: PI = IR(10 min) ÷ IR(1 min). Interpret the result according to transformer design, insulation system, temperature and applicable testing guidance. A PI of 2 or higher is not a universal acceptance criterion.

When should transformer oil filtration be carried out?+

Oil filtration or dehydration may be required when test results indicate excessive moisture or suspended contamination. Vacuum treatment may also be needed for moisture and dissolved-gas removal. Oil reclamation or replacement may be considered when chemical ageing is significant. Select the treatment based on laboratory results and OEM recommendations.

Why is transformer thermography performed?+

Infrared thermography helps identify abnormal temperature patterns at bushings, terminals, cable connections, joints, radiators, cooling fans and other accessible components. It is most useful when performed under suitable operating load and interpreted by a competent person.

Why is winding resistance testing important?+

Winding resistance testing helps identify high-resistance joints, loose connections, broken conductor strands and abnormalities in winding or tap-changer connections. Compare phase and tap readings after correcting for winding temperature and following the test-equipment manufacturer’s procedure.

What should be checked before energising a transformer after maintenance?+

Important checks include:

  • Verify all electrical connections
  • Confirm correct oil level
  • Check radiator and cooling valve positions
  • Verify tank and neutral earthing
  • Confirm protection relays and trip circuits are restored
  • Remove temporary test connections and earths according to the approved procedure
  • Verify OLTC tap position
  • Complete the pre-energisation checklist
  • Obtain switching authorisation
  • Monitor voltage, current, temperature and abnormal noise after energisation

19. Key Takeaways

  • Combine regular visual inspection, preventive mechanical maintenance, oil monitoring, electrical diagnostics and protection verification.
  • Use condition-based maintenance and trend every result.
  • Early detection of insulation deterioration, moisture, abnormal gas, cooling defects and mechanical problems cuts failure risk.
  • Adapt the schedule to design, environment, loading, monitoring results and OEM recommendations.
